# Bacterial Leaf Blight in Rice (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)

## Symptoms

Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB) is one of the most destructive diseases of rice in South and Southeast Asia. The disease is caused by *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae*.

**Early symptoms** include water-soaked lesions on leaf margins, starting from the tips. These lesions are initially small, then elongate along the veins, turning yellow and eventually white-grey. In severe infections, the entire leaf may wilt and die.

**Key diagnostic indicators:**
- Water-soaked, translucent streaks along leaf veins
- Yellow to straw-coloured leaf tips and margins
- Milky or yellowish bacterial ooze from cut lesions (visible in early morning)
- Leaves drying out from tip downward in a "V" pattern

## Conditions Favouring the Disease

BLB thrives under conditions of high humidity (above 70%), excessive nitrogen fertilization, and continuous standing water in the field. The disease is most severe during the vegetative and early reproductive stages. High planting density, wounds from wind or insects, and rainy weather all increase vulnerability.

## Severity Staging

- **Early**: Yellow tips on upper leaves, fewer than 20% of leaves affected
- **Moderate**: 20–50% of leaves showing lesions, some leaf death
- **Severe**: More than 50% of leaves affected, significant yield loss expected

## Management

### Cultural Controls
1. **Drain the field** — reduce standing water to lower humidity at the canopy level
2. **Stop nitrogen top-dressing** — excess nitrogen promotes succulent growth that BLB bacteria exploit
3. **Avoid movement through wet fields** — workers and implements spread the bacterium
4. **Use resistant varieties** where available (e.g., Improved Samba Mahsuri, CO 51)

### Chemical Controls
1. Apply **copper-based bactericide** (Copper Hydroxide 77% WP at 2.5 g/L) as foliar spray
2. Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline mixture (300 ppm) may be used as a last resort, following local regulations
3. Spray at weekly intervals during active infection

### Biological Controls
- Pseudomonas fluorescens (talc-based formulation, 10 g/L) as a prophylactic spray

## When to Escalate to Expert

Escalate to KVK agronomist when:
- Symptoms spread to more than 30% of the canopy despite 7 days of cultural control
- The farmer reports "got worse" on follow-up after treatment
- BLB appears alongside another infection (compound problem)
