# Rice Blast Disease (Magnaporthe oryzae)

## Symptoms

Rice blast, caused by *Magnaporthe oryzae*, is the most destructive fungal disease of rice worldwide. It can affect leaves, nodes, necks, and panicles.

**Key diagnostic features:**
- **Leaf blast**: Diamond-shaped or spindle-shaped lesions with grey centres and dark brown borders. Young lesions appear as small white to grey-green spots.
- **Neck blast**: Infection at the panicle base causing "broken neck" — the panicle bends and turns white, grains are empty or shrivelled.
- **Node blast**: Dark brown to black discoloration at nodes, causing stem breakage.

## Conditions Favouring the Disease

Blast thrives in cool nights (20–25°C), warm days, prolonged leaf wetness (dew), and high nitrogen fertilization. Upland and rainfed areas are more vulnerable than irrigated lowlands. Dense plant populations increase canopy humidity and disease pressure.

## Management

### Cultural Controls
1. Avoid excessive nitrogen — split applications, do not exceed 100 kg N/ha
2. Maintain adequate spacing (20 × 15 cm) for air circulation
3. Use resistant varieties (e.g., CO 51, ADT 43 in Tamil Nadu)
4. Remove and destroy infected plant debris after harvest

### Chemical Controls
1. Tricyclazole 75% WP at 0.6 g/L — most effective preventive fungicide for blast
2. Isoprothiolane 40% EC at 1.5 mL/L as curative treatment
3. Carbendazim 50% WP at 1 g/L for moderate cases

### When to Escalate
Escalate immediately if neck blast is observed — yield loss can exceed 50% and intervention must be rapid.
