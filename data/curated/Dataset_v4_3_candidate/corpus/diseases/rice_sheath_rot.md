# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Sheath Rot

## 1. Overview & Crop Stages Affected
Sheath rot, caused by *Sarocladium oryzae*, attacks the uppermost flag leaf sheath enclosing the young panicle during the booting to heading stages. The disease is frequently aggravated by stem borer or mite damage to the sheath.

## 2. Distinguishing Field Identification Cues
- Irregular, oblong, or oblong-elliptical spots on the flag leaf sheath with grey-white centers and dark brown margins.
- Young panicles fail to emerge completely from the boot leaf (a condition known as "boot choking").
- Abundant white powdery fungal mycelium and conidia visible on the inner surface of the leaf sheath.
- Grains in affected panicles turn dark reddish-brown to dark grey and remain sterile or poorly filled.

## 3. Integrated Disease Management
- **Avoid Insect Wounding**: Manage stem borers and earhead mites whose wounds facilitate *Sarocladium* entry.
- **Fungicidal Interventions (Validated)**:
  - *Carbendazim 50 WP* @ 500 g/ha (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`).
  - *Propiconazole 25 EC* @ 500 ml/ha (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`).
