# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Rice Tungro Disease (RTV)

## 1. Overview & Crop Stages Affected
Rice Tungro Disease is caused by a composite infection of two distinct viruses:
1. **Rice Tungro Bacilliform Virus (RTBV)**: Causes disease symptoms (stunting and yellowing).
2. **Rice Tungro Spherical Virus (RTSV)**: Required for insect vector acquisition and transmission.

The disease is transmitted in a semi-persistent manner primarily by the **Green Leafhopper (*Nephotettix virescens*)**. Plants are most vulnerable during the vegetative phase from nursery stage up to 45 days after transplanting.

## 2. Distinguishing Field Identification Cues
- Marked stunting of hills and drastically reduced tiller count.
- Leaves turn yellow to orange-yellow or deep rusty orange, starting from the tip and progressing downward along leaf margins.
- Youngest leaves may exhibit mottled patterns, interveinal chlorosis, and rust-like necrotic spots.
- Root system is poorly developed and dark brown, resulting in poor anchorage.
- Panicles are small, sterile, poorly exerted, and bear dark brown blotched grains.

## 3. Iodine Starch Test (Field Diagnostic Test)
- Cut a 5 cm section of the discolored leaf blade in early morning.
- Immerse in 10% alcohol to decolorize, then place in 2% Iodine-Potassium Iodide ($I_2-KI$) solution for 5 minutes.
- Tungro-infected leaves turn deep blue/black due to abnormal starch accumulation in blocked phloem, whereas non-viral nutritional yellowing remains brown.

## 4. Integrated Disease Management
- **Resistant Varieties**: Grow Tungro-resistant varieties such as CO 51, ADT 43, and ASD 16.
- **Vector Control**: Suppress Green Leafhopper vector populations in nursery and main field before transmission occurs:
  - *Imidacloprid 17.8 SL* @ 100–125 ml/ha (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`).
  - *Thiamethoxam 25 WG* @ 100 g/ha (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`).
- **Sanitation**: Rogue and destroy infected hills in early stages; eliminate grassy weed reservoirs (*Leersia*, *Echinochloa*).
