# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Stem Rot

## 1. Overview & Agro-Ecological Conditions
Stem rot, caused by *Sclerotium oryzae*, is a soil-borne fungal disease prevalent in ill-drained lowland paddy fields with stagnant water, heavy clay soils, and unbalanced high-nitrogen fertilization.

## 2. Distinguishing Field Diagnostic Cues
- **Waterline Lesions**: Initial symptoms appear during late tillering as small, dark blackish-brown irregular lesions on outer leaf sheaths at the water surface.
- **Culm Decay**: As the fungus penetrates the culm, the stem turns soft, discolored, and hollow.
- **Sclerotial Bodies**: When the rotting stem is split open, numerous tiny, black, spherical sclerotia (like mustard seeds) are visible inside the hollow culm cavity.
- **Premature Lodging**: Infected tillers snap and lodge near the base before harvest, resulting in unfilled or partially filled chalky grains.

## 3. Integrated Agronomic Management
1. **Water Management (Crucial)**: Drain standing water periodically to expose the soil and stem base to sunlight and oxygen. Intermittent wetting and drying (AWD) prevents sclerotial accumulation.
2. **Nutritional Balance**: Apply potash (Potassium) in split doses to harden the silicon layer of the culm. Avoid applying top-dress nitrogen when disease is active.
3. **Fungicidal Interventions (Targeted Base Application)**:
   - *Hexaconazole 5 EC* @ 1000 ml/ha (2 ml/L water) directed at the plant base.
   - *Validamycin 3 L* @ 1000 ml/ha directed at the waterline.
