# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Brown Spot

## 1. Identification
Brown spot, caused by the fungal pathogen *Bipolaris oryzae* (teleomorph *Cochliobolus miyabeanus*, formerly *Helminthosporium oryzae*), is a major seed-borne and airborne disease of rice, historically notorious as a major cause of the Great Bengal Famine of 1943.

Farmers and field scouts can identify the disease by its characteristic foliar and grain symptoms:
- **Leaf Lesions**: Typical spots are **circular to oval, resembling sesame seeds** (0.5 to 3.0 mm long). They have a uniform dark brown or reddish-brown color in early stages.
- **Mature Spots with Halo**: As lesions mature, the center becomes greyish-white or light brown, surrounded by a distinct dark reddish-brown margin and a prominent bright **yellow chlorotic halo**.
- **Glume & Grain Discoloration**: Infected glumes develop dark brown to blackish spots covering part or all of the husk, resulting in shriveled, discolored, and chalky grains.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate differential diagnosis prevents confusion with other foliar diseases:
- **Brown Spot vs. Rice Blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae*)**: Brown spot lesions are **oval or circular with rounded ends (sesame seed shape)** and prominent yellow halos. Blast lesions are distinctly **spindle-shaped with pointed tapering ends** and ash-grey centers.
- **Brown Spot vs. Zinc / Iron Nutritional Deficiency**: Brown spot lesions are discrete, scattered spots with defined brown margins and fungal conidia visible under magnification. Zinc deficiency produces diffuse, irregular rusty-brown blotches starting from the base of middle leaves without defined circular margins.
- **Brown Spot vs. Bacterial Leaf Blight**: BLB forms expanding marginal lesions with wavy edges, whereas brown spot forms discrete oval spots scattered across the entire leaf blade.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Seedling Blight**: Infected seeds fail to germinate or produce stunted seedlings with brown lesions on the coleoptile, causing seedling rot and mortality.
- **Foliar Blighting & Yellowing**: In heavily infected fields, hundreds of spots coalesce on each leaf, causing premature leaf yellowing and complete drying of the foliage.
- **Grain Discoloration (Dirty Panicle Syndrome)**: Fungal invasion of glumes causes black discoloration and internal grain chalkiness, drastically reducing milling recovery and seed viability.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Predisposing Soil Factors (Poor Man's Disease)**: Brown spot is strongly correlated with **poor soil fertility**, nutrient imbalances (especially nitrogen, potassium, and silica deficits), zinc/manganese deficiency, saline-alkaline soils, and drought/moisture stress.
- **Primary & Secondary Spread**: Primary infection originates from seed-borne mycelium and infected soil debris; secondary spread occurs rapidly through wind-borne multicelled brown cylindrical conidia.
- **Environmental Factors**: Favored by temperatures between $25\text{–}30^\circ\text{C}$, relative humidity $>85\%$, cloudy weather, and prolonged leaf wetness.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
- **Nursery / Seedling Stage**: Seedling blight and foliar spotting in dry nursery beds.
- **Active Tillering Stage**: Foliar spotting appears on older leaves; indicator of soil nutritional stress.
- **Booting to Grain Filling Stage**: Infection moves to flag leaves and glumes, directly degrading grain quality.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Standard numerical ETLs are not established for brown spot).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: Correct underlying soil nutritional stress (potash/zinc) and apply fungicidal foliar treatment when **$\ge 5\%\text{ of leaf area}$ is covered by brown spots** during the tillering to booting phase.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Small dark brown oval spots on $<5\%$ of lower leaves; no glume infection.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Oval spots ($2\text{–}5\text{ mm}$) with grey centers and yellow halos on $6\text{–}10\%$ of leaf area.
  - *Severe / Spreading*: Coalescing spots on $>11\text{–}25\%$ of leaf area with yellowing and drying of upper leaves.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>25\%$ leaf area necrotic; heavy glume spotting and dirty panicles.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for Brown Spot)*:
    - SES 1–3: $<1\%\text{–}5\%$ leaf area with small oval spots (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}10\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $11\%\text{–}25\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>25\%$ leaf area affected with severe glume spotting (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Soil Fertility Correction (Primary Solution)**: Apply balanced NPK fertilizers; correct potassium deficiency by applying muriate of potash (MOP @ 25 kg/ha). Apply zinc sulphate @ 25 kg/ha at basal land preparation.
- **Seed Treatment (Crucial)**: Treat seeds before sowing with Carbendazim 50 WP @ 2 g/kg seed, Mancozeb 75 WP @ 2 g/kg, or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 10 g/kg seed to eliminate seed-borne inoculum.
- **Organic Amendments**: Apply well-decomposed Farmyard Manure (FYM @ 12.5 t/ha) or green leaf manuring (Daincha) to improve soil organic carbon and water-holding capacity.
- **Water Management**: Avoid moisture stress; maintain adequate soil moisture throughout tillering and grain filling.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Seed & Soil Biocontrol**: Treat seeds with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 10 g/kg and apply soil application of *Trichoderma viride* @ 2.5 kg/ha mixed in 50 kg FYM.
- **Foliar Bio-Protection**: Spray *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water at early tillering and panicle emergence.

## 10. Chemical Management
When foliar spotting begins to spread or during boot leaf stage in nutrient-deficient soils, apply approved fungicides in 500 L water/ha:
- **Mancozeb 75 WP** @ 1000 g/ha (2 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). CIBRC registered contact dithiocarbamate fungicide; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 15 days.
- **Carbendazim 12% + Mancozeb 63% WP (75 WP)** @ 750 g/ha (1.5 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Dual-action systemic and contact combination; PHI: 30 days.
- **Propiconazole 25 EC** @ 500 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Triazole fungicide providing systemic foliar protection.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Lower Leaf Inspection**: Check the lowest 2 to 3 leaves during tillering for early oval spots.
- **Soil Status Correlation**: If brown spot occurs uniformly across a plot, test soil for zinc and potassium deficiencies.
- **Panicle Inspection**: Inspect maturing panicles for dark brown spots on spikelet husks.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the Block Agricultural Extension Officer or KVK Soil / Plant Pathologist if:
- **Extensive Glume Blackening**: Severe grain spotting affects $>20\%$ of emerging panicles.
- **Nutrient-Associated Crop Failure**: Severe spotting accompanied by complete stunting across sandy or saline parcels.
