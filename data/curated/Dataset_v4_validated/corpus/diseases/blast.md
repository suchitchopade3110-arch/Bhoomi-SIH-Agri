# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Rice Blast

## 1. Identification
Rice Blast, caused by the filamentous ascomycete fungus *Magnaporthe oryzae* (anamorph *Pyricularia oryzae*), is universally recognized as the most devastating airborne fungal disease of rice, capable of causing total crop failure under severe epidemic conditions.

Farmers and field scouts can identify the disease across its anatomical infection forms:
- **Leaf Blast (இலை குலை நோய்)**: Typically appears during nursery, tillering, and vegetative phases. Characteristic lesions are **spindle-shaped (eye-shaped or diamond-shaped)** with pointed ends, featuring a greyish-white or ash-colored center surrounded by a dark brown to reddish-brown necrotic border and often a chlorotic yellow halo.
- **Node Blast (கணு குலை நோய்)**: Blackish-brown necrotic lesions encircle the stem culm nodes, causing the stem to become brittle and break easily under wind pressure.
- **Neck Blast / Rotten Neck (கழுத்து குலை நோய்)**: The base of the panicle rachis (neck node) is infected just prior to or during panicle emergence, turning dark brown to black. The vascular supply is severed, causing the panicle to snap and droop, producing 100% empty, chaffy white earheads.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate field differential diagnosis is vital:
- **Leaf Blast vs. Brown Spot (*Bipolaris oryzae*)**: Leaf blast lesions are distinctly **spindle-shaped (eye-shaped) with pointed tapering ends** and greyish-white centers. Brown spot lesions are **oval or circular, resembling sesame seeds**, with uniform brown coloration and lacking pointed spindle ends.
- **Neck Blast vs. Stem Borer White Earhead**: In neck blast, the panicle exhibits a **dark brown or blackish necrotic girdle** at the panicle neck base, and the panicle remains firmly attached to the culm until mechanically broken. In stem borer damage, the white earhead pulls out effortlessly with chewed frass at the base and no black neck lesion.
- **Node Blast vs. Stem Rot**: Node blast lesions are hard, black, and strictly localized to culm nodes, whereas stem rot (*Sclerotium oryzae*) rots the leaf sheath and culm base internally near the water line with visible black sclerotia.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Spindle Lesion Coalescence**: On susceptible cultivars, hundreds of eye-shaped lesions coalesce rapidly, turning entire leaf blades brown and dry, producing a characteristic "blast-scorched" or burned canopy appearance.
- **Rotten Neck & Lodging**: When neck infection occurs at panicle emergence, grains fail to fill entirely, and panicles turn bleached white and snap at the neck joint.
- **Conidial Fluff**: Under high morning humidity, a delicate greyish-velvety fungal down (conidiophores and conidia) is visible on the surface of young active lesions.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Spore Germination & Penetration**: Airborne pyriform (pear-shaped) three-celled conidia land on leaf surfaces, germinate within hours in free moisture, form specialized melanized appressoria, and penetrate epidermal cells directly using high turgor pressure.
- **Rapid Polycyclic Epidemics**: A single lesion produces 2,000 to 6,000 conidia per night for over two weeks, allowing explosive field-wide epidemic buildup within 7 to 10 days.
- **Survival**: Overwinters as mycelium and conidia in infected crop residues, seeds, and alternate grassy weed hosts (*Digitaria sanguinalis*, *Echinochloa*, *Panicum repens*).
- **Environmental Triggers**: Cool night temperatures ($18\text{–}24^\circ\text{C}$), high relative humidity ($>90\%$), prolonged dew periods ($\ge 10\text{ hours}$), overcast rainy days, and heavy application of nitrogenous fertilizers greatly stimulate blast infection.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
- **Nursery / Seedling Stage**: Severe leaf blast causing seedling damping-off and mortality.
- **Active Tillering Stage**: Leaf blast peaks; rapid defoliation of middle canopy.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Highly vulnerable window where fungus moves to flag leaves.
- **Heading / Panicle Emergence Stage (Critical Danger Zone)**: Peak susceptibility for catastrophic **neck blast**, resulting in complete grain sterility.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Standard insect-like numerical ETLs do not apply to airborne blast epidemics).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**:
  - *Vegetative Stage*: $\ge 1\text{ to }5\%\text{ leaf area affected}$ with spindle-shaped lesions in lower canopy.
  - *Reproductive Stage*: **Immediate preventive intervention** at late booting (50% boot leaf stage) prior to panicle emergence if weather is humid and cool, or if leaf blast was present during tillering.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Small pinhead brown specks or isolated small spindle lesions on $<5\%$ of lower leaves.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Typical spindle-shaped lesions ($0.5\text{–}1.5\text{ cm}$) on $6\text{–}10\%$ of leaf area.
  - *Severe / Critical*: Coalescing lesions on $>11\text{–}25\%$ of leaf area OR initial dark neck blast lesions developing at panicle node.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>25\%$ canopy scorched OR $>10\%$ panicles showing rotten neck.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for Rice Blast)*:
    - SES 1–3: $<1\%\text{–}5\%$ leaf area with small spindle lesions (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}10\%$ leaf area with typical spindle lesions (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $11\%\text{–}25\%$ leaf area affected or initial neck blast (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>25\%$ leaf area affected or $>10\%$ rotten neck blast (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant & Tolerant Cultivars**: Cultivate blast-resistant regional cultivars, such as CO 51, ADT 45, TPS 5, and Improved Samba Mahsuri.
- **Balanced Nitrogen Fertilization**: Avoid high single-dose urea applications; apply nitrogen in 3 to 4 balanced splits. Apply silicon fertilizers or potassium silicate to increase leaf cuticle hardness against fungal penetration.
- **Adjusted Sowing Dates**: Avoid late planting that forces heading into cool, foggy winter months (November–December).
- **Weed Sanitation**: Remove collateral weed hosts (*Digitaria*, *Echinochloa*) from bunds and water channels.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Seed Treatment with Biocontrol Agents**: Treat seeds with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 10 g/kg seed or *Trichoderma viride* @ 4 g/kg seed.
- **Foliar Bio-Protection**: Apply foliar spray of *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (liquid formulation @ 2.5 L/ha or talc powder @ 2.5 kg/ha) at early tillering and boot leaf stages.

## 10. Chemical Management
When leaf blast lesions appear or when heading occurs in blast-prone weather, apply validated systemic fungicides in 500 L water/ha:
- **Tricyclazole 75 WP** @ 500 g/ha (0.6 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). The gold-standard blasticide; highly systemic; prevents appressorial melanization and fungal penetration; highly effective for both leaf and neck blast; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days.
- **Isoprothiolane 40 EC** @ 750 ml/ha (1.5 ml/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Systemic blasticide; inhibits lipid biosynthesis; PHI: 30 days.
- **Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC (29.6 SC)** @ 500 ml/ha (1 ml/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Dual-action systemic fungicide providing preventive and curative control; PHI: 31 days.
- **Kasugamycin 3% SL** @ 1000–1250 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Antibiotic fungicide derived from *Streptomyces kasugaensis*; CIBRC approved for blast.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Cool Dew Inspection**: Scout fields between 6:00 and 8:00 AM on overcast, cool mornings; inspect lower and middle leaves for young spindle-shaped lesions.
- **Boot Leaf Node Scouting**: Inspect the panicle neck collar as panicles emerge from the boot.
- **Spore Trap Data**: Pay attention to regional agromet advisories warning of airborne blast spore surges.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Plant Pathologist if:
- **Neck Blast at Panicle Emergence**: Appearance of black neck girdles during 50% panicle emergence requires emergency targeted systemic fungicide spray within 24 hours.
- **Resistance Breakdown in Primary Cultivars**: Widespread severe leaf blast on previously resistant varieties.
- **Fungicide Non-Response**: Spindle lesions expanding 4 days after Tricyclazole application.
