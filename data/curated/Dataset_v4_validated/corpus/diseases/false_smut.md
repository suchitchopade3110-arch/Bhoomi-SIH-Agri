# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: False Smut

## 1. Identification
False smut, caused by the clavicipitaceous ascomycete fungus *Ustilaginoidea virens* (teleomorph *Villosiclava virens*), is an economically damaging grain-infecting fungal disease of rice, increasingly prevalent in modern high-yielding, heavily fertilized irrigated rice tracts.

Farmers and field scouts can identify false smut by its unmistakable visual symptoms on the mature panicle:
- **Velvety Smut Balls (பூஞ்சாண உருண்டைகள்)**: Individual grains in a panicle are transformed into large, spherical, velvety or powdery spore balls measuring 1.0 to 1.5 cm or more in diameter.
- **Color Progression**: 
  - *Immature Stage*: Smut balls are smooth, yellow to orange-yellow, enclosed in a thin membrane.
  - *Mature Stage*: The membrane ruptures, and the balls turn dark olive-green, greenish-black, or velvety black, releasing powdery chlamydospores.
- **Scattered Spikelet Infection**: Usually only 4 to 10 individual grains per panicle are transformed, while the remaining grains appear normal; however, adjacent healthy grains and straw become contaminated with toxic spores and mycotoxins (*ustiloxins*).

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate field diagnosis is straightforward due to the distinct morphology of false smut balls:
- **False Smut vs. Bunt / Kernel Smut (*Tilletia barclayana*)**: False smut forms **large, velvety, greenish-yellow to blackish balls** that swell far beyond the glumes (up to 1 cm). Kernel smut/bunt stays concealed inside the grain hull, producing a black powdery mass that replaces the endosperm without large external velvety balls.
- **False Smut vs. Insect Galls**: Gall midge creates vegetative onion-leaf galls at the tiller base during tillering, whereas false smut infects strictly individual floral spikelets on the panicle during flowering.
- **False Smut vs. Udbatta Disease (*Ephelis oryzae*)**: Udbatta disease transforms the entire panicle into a single cylindrical, greyish-white chalky cylinder resembling an incense stick (*agarbatti*), whereas false smut affects only individual scattered grains.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Floral Transformation into Spore Balls**: The fungal mycelium invades the young ovary during flowering, completely replacing the developing grain tissues with a dense mass of mycelium and chlamydospores.
- **Chaffiness of Adjacent Spikelets**: Spikelets adjacent to infected smut balls fail to fill normally and become sterile or chaffy due to nutrient diversion.
- **Mycotoxin Contamination (Ustiloxins)**: The fungus produces mycotoxins known as *ustiloxins* and *ustilaginoidins*, which pose health risks to livestock and humans, leading to grain consignment rejections and reduced seed germination.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Infection Pathway**: Airborne ascospores or chlamydospores infect the spikelet during the booting and early flowering stages, penetrating the ovary before pollination or immediately after fertilization.
- **Survival**: Survives as hard black sclerotia and thick-walled chlamydospores in the soil, on crop stubble, and as seed contaminants. Sclerotia float on water surfaces and germinate to produce airborne ascospores.
- **Epidemiological Triggers**: High relative humidity ($>90\%$), rainfall or frequent cloudy drizzle during the **booting to flowering stages**, moderate temperatures ($25\text{–}30^\circ\text{C}$), and high nitrogen fertilizer application create severe epidemic outbreaks.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
False smut infection exhibits a strictly time-sensitive vulnerability window:
- **Vegetative Phase (Seedling to Tillering)**: Not susceptible.
- **Booting Stage (50% Boot Leaf Stage)**: **The single critical window for infection and management.**
- **Flowering / Anthesis Stage**: Infection establishes inside young ovaries.
- **Milking & Maturation Stage**: Visible emergence of velvety yellow-green smut balls. *Note: Fungicide application after smut balls are visible is completely ineffective.*

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Post-infection thresholds cannot be used because visible symptoms occur too late for curative treatment).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: **Mandatory preventive fungicidal application at the 50% boot leaf stage (prior to panicle emergence)** in fields with a history of false smut or when high humidity and overcast rains coincide with booting.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: $<1\%$ of panicles with 1 to 2 yellow smut balls per panicle.
  - *Moderate Severity*: $2\text{–}5\%$ of panicles affected with 3 to 5 smut balls per panicle.
  - *Severe / Spreading*: $>6\text{–}15\%$ of panicles affected with multiple large greenish-black balls.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>15\%$ of panicles heavily contaminated; severe grain discoloration and rejection.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for False Smut)*:
    - SES 1–3: $<1\%$ panicles affected with 1–2 smut balls/panicle (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $2\%\text{–}5\%$ panicles affected with 3–5 smut balls/panicle (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $6\%\text{–}15\%$ panicles affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>15\%$ panicles affected with heavy spore contamination (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Field Sanitation & Sclerotia Removal**: Hand-pick and destroy smut balls in small cloth bags before harvest to prevent sclerotia and spores from falling onto the soil.
- **Deep Summer Plowing**: Deeply plow fields after harvest to bury surface sclerotia below 15 cm, preventing ascospore emergence.
- **Balanced Nitrogen Management**: Avoid excess urea top-dressing during late vegetative and booting stages.
- **Synchronized Sowing**: Adjust planting dates so that crop heading does not coincide with peak monsoon rainfall.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Seed Treatment**: Treat seeds with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 10 g/kg seed or *Trichoderma viride* @ 4 g/kg seed to suppress seed-borne spores.
- **Foliar Biocontrol**: Spray *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 2.5 kg/ha at boot leaf stage to colonize spikelets preventively.

## 10. Chemical Management
**Critical Timing Rule**: Fungicide application must be completed strictly at the **booting stage (50% boot leaf stage, before panicle emergence)**. Once smut balls are visible, chemical sprays cannot reverse or cure the infection.
- **Propiconazole 25 EC** @ 500 ml/ha (1 ml/L water) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly effective systemic triazole fungicide; spray at boot leaf stage; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days.
- **Copper Hydroxide 77 WP** @ 1.25 kg/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Broad-spectrum contact copper bactericide/fungicide; apply at late boot stage.
- **Prohibited Anthesis Spraying**: Never spray chemicals during open morning flowering (anthesis, 9:00 to 11:30 AM) to avoid chemical floret blast, pollen washing, and spikelet sterility.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Boot Stage Weather Tracking**: Track weather forecasts at the booting stage; 3 or more consecutive cloudy/rainy days with $>90\%\text{ RH}$ indicate high risk requiring preventive boot spray.
- **Harvest Inspection**: Inspect mature panicles across 100 hills for velvety smut balls.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Agronomist if:
- **Widespread False Smut Contamination**: Over 10% of panicles show smut balls across commercial seed production plots (requires seed lot rejection).
- **Misguided Post-Emergence Spraying**: Prevent farmers from spraying expensive systemic fungicides after panicles have emerged, as it causes financial loss with zero therapeutic benefit.
