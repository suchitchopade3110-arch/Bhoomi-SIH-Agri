# TNAU & IRRI Rice Disease Management Guide: Sheath Blight

## 1. Identification
Sheath blight, caused by the necrotrophic soil-borne sclerotial fungus *Rhizoctonia solani* (teleomorph *Thanatephorus cucumeris*, anastomosis group AG-1 IA), is one of the most widespread and economically damaging fungal diseases of irrigated rice in Tamil Nadu and South India.

Farmers and field scouts can identify sheath blight through its distinct lesions and progression along the stem culm:
- **Water-Soaked Lower Sheath Lesions**: Initial lesions appear on the lower leaf sheaths just 2 to 5 cm above the waterline as oval, oblong, or elliptical greenish-grey, water-soaked spots (1.0 to 3.0 cm long).
- **Snake-Skin Pattern (பாம்பு தோல் வடிவம்)**: As lesions mature and expand, they develop dark reddish-brown to purple-brown margins surrounding a bleached, straw-colored or greyish-white center. Multiple lesions coalesce to form an irregular, banded "snake-skin" or marbled pattern.
- **Sclerotia Formation**: Small, spherical, globose fungal structures (**sclerotia**, resembling white to dark brown mustard seeds, 1.0 to 2.5 mm in diameter) form abundantly on the surface of lesions and inside the sheath.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate field differential diagnosis is essential:
- **Sheath Blight vs. Sheath Rot (*Sarocladium oryzae*)**: Sheath blight initiates on **lower leaf sheaths near the waterline during tillering** and forms broad, oval, banded snake-skin lesions with prominent brown mustard-like sclerotia. Sheath rot attacks strictly the **uppermost flag leaf sheath enclosing the young panicle during booting**, forming greyish-brown oblong lesions with powdery white fungal growth and causing panicle choking.
- **Sheath Blight vs. Stem Rot (*Sclerotium oryzae*)**: Sheath blight infects the outer leaf sheaths with broad greenish-grey lesions and external brown sclerotia. Stem rot attacks the culm internally at the water line, causing the stem culm to rot, turn black, and collapse with tiny black pinhead-like sclerotia inside the hollow culm.
- **Sheath Blight vs. Aggregated Sheath Spot (*Ceratobasidium oryzae-sativae*)**: Aggregated sheath spot produces smaller, discrete oval lesions with broad brown margins that rarely climb above the middle canopy, whereas *Rhizoctonia solani* climbs aggressively to the flag leaf.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Vertical Disease Progression (Lesion Height)**: The fungus climbs vertically from the lower sheaths to the middle leaves, upper sheaths, and eventually infects the flag leaf blade and panicle collar.
- **Canopy Blighting & Lodging**: When infection reaches the upper canopy, leaf sheaths rot, photosynthetic tissue is destroyed, and the base of tillers weakens, leading to severe field lodging.
- **Incomplete Panicle Exertion**: Infection of the flag leaf collar chokes panicle emergence, producing chaffy, sterile grains.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Primary Inoculum (Floating Sclerotia)**: Sclerotia survive in the soil and crop debris for up to two years. When fields are flooded and puddled, sclerotia float on the water surface, adhere to emerging rice tillers, and penetrate epidermal cells via infection cushions.
- **Secondary Spread (Canopy Contact)**: Spread occurs through thick runner hyphae growing vertically along the tiller and horizontally to adjacent hills via touching leaves in dense, heavily fertilized canopies.
- **Epidemiological Triggers**: High relative humidity ($95\text{–}100\%$), warm temperatures ($28\text{–}32^\circ\text{C}$), high planting density, standing water, and excessive nitrogen application.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
- **Nursery Stage**: Rarely affected under normal conditions.
- **Active Tillering to Maximum Tillering**: Sclerotia initiate infection at the waterline; lesions establish on lower sheaths.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: **Peak vertical spread phase**; fungus climbs to upper sheaths.
- **Flowering & Grain Filling Stage**: Culmination of damage; flag leaf blighting, lodging, and yield loss.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Expressed via Relative Lesion Height index rather than insect count).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: Initiate fungicidal application when **$\ge 5\text{ to }10\%\text{ of hills}$ show water-soaked lesions on lower sheaths** during the maximum tillering to panicle initiation stages.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Greenish-grey lesions restricted to lowest leaf sheaths near the waterline (Relative Lesion Height $\le 20\%$).
  - *Moderate Severity*: Lesions climbing up to 3rd or 4th leaf sheath ($21\%\text{–}40\%\text{ RLH}$).
  - *Severe / Critical*: Lesions reaching the flag leaf sheath and collar ($41\%\text{–}60\%\text{ RLH}$) with abundant brown sclerotia.
  - *Catastrophic*: Lesions covering $>60\%\text{ RLH}$, spreading into flag leaf blade and panicle with severe lodging.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Relative Lesion Height (RLH) Index & SES Scale (IRRI/TNAU for Sheath Blight)*:
    - SES 1–3: $\text{RLH} \le 20\%$ (lower sheaths near waterline) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $\text{RLH } 21\%\text{–}40\%$ (reaching 3rd/4th leaf sheath) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $\text{RLH } 41\%\text{–}60\%$ (reaching flag leaf sheath) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $\text{RLH } > 60\%$ (reaching flag leaf blade and panicle) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Wider Spacing**: Avoid dense planting; maintain spacing of $20\text{ cm} \times 15\text{ cm}$ or $20\text{ cm} \times 20\text{ cm}$ to allow light and aeration into the lower canopy.
- **Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)**: Drain standing water for 2 to 3 days periodically during tillering to reduce microclimatic humidity at the tiller base.
- **Skimming Floating Sclerotia**: During puddling and land leveling, skim off floating crop residues and sclerotia with cloth nets before transplanting.
- **Balanced Nutrition**: Avoid excess urea; split nitrogen into 3 equal doses. Apply potassium (potash @ 25 kg/ha) and zinc.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Pseudomonas fluorescens / Trichoderma viride**: Apply talc-based formulation of *Pseudomonas fluorescens* or *Trichoderma viride* @ 2.5 kg/ha mixed with 50 kg well-decomposed FYM at transplanting, followed by foliar spray @ 2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water at 45 DAT.
- **Soil Organic Amendments**: Incorporate neem cake @ 250 kg/ha or green manure to stimulate antagonistic soil actinomycetes.

## 10. Chemical Management
When lesions appear on lower sheaths during tillering or booting, apply approved fungicides directed specifically at the **base of tillers** in 500 L water/ha:
- **Hexaconazole 5 SC / 5 EC** @ 1000 ml/ha (2 ml/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly systemic triazole fungicide; direct spray to lower plant base; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days.
- **Validamycin 3 L** @ 1000 ml/ha (2 ml/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Antibiotic/fungicide derived from *Streptomyces hygroscopicus*; inhibits trehalase enzyme; extremely safe to spiders and beneficial fauna; PHI: 14 days.
- **Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC (29.6 SC)** @ 500 ml/ha (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Broad-spectrum systemic combination; PHI: 31 days.
- **Thifluzamide 24 SC** @ 375 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Succinate dehydrogenase inhibitor (SDHI); highly effective against *Rhizoctonia*.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Base of Tiller Scouting**: Part the crop canopy and inspect the lowest leaf sheaths near the water level across 20 fixed hills per acre once every 4 days between tillering and flowering.
- **Track Vertical Climb (RLH)**: Measure the height of the topmost lesion relative to total plant height to calculate Relative Lesion Height ($\text{RLH} = \frac{\text{Lesion Height}}{\text{Plant Height}} \times 100$).

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Plant Pathologist if:
- **Rapid Vertical Climb**: Lesions advance from lower waterline sheaths to the flag leaf collar within 5 days under humid weather.
- **Widespread Field Lodging**: Tillers rotting at base and lodging in circular patches during early grain fill.
- **Fungicide Ineffectiveness**: Lesions continue vertical ascent 5 days after Hexaconazole or Validamycin application.
