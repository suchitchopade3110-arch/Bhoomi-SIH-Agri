# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Bacterial Leaf Streak (BLS)

## 1. Identification
Bacterial Leaf Streak (BLS), caused by the parenchymatous bacterial pathogen *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzicola*, is an important foliar disease of rice in high-rainfall tropical and subtropical tracts.

Farmers and field scouts can identify the disease through its specific foliar manifestations:
- **Interveinal Streaks**: Narrow, fine, water-soaked, translucent brownish-yellow streaks initiating strictly between the longitudinal veins of the leaf blade.
- **Bacterial Beads & Crusts**: Small, spherical, yellow to amber-colored bacterial exudate beads form abundantly along the streaks under high humidity, drying into tiny hard resinous beads that reflect sunlight.
- **Transmitted Light Appearance**: When an infected leaf is held up against the sun or sky, the lesions appear bright, translucent golden-yellow, clearly delimited by parallel veins.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate field differentiation between BLS and other foliar disorders is critical:
- **BLS vs. Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB)**: BLS forms **narrow, straight interveinal streaks** restricted between leaf veins with tiny amber beads. BLB (*Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae*) attacks the vascular system, forming broad marginal lesions with **wavy borders** that start at leaf tips and cause systemic wilting (*Kresek*). BLS never causes vascular systemic wilting or Kresek.
- **BLS vs. Rice Leaf Streak Fungal Spot**: Fungal streaks are darker, irregular, lack the characteristic amber bacterial exudate beads, and do not show bright translucency under transmitted sunlight.
- **BLS vs. Physiological Drought Streak**: Drought stress causes leaf tips to roll and wither uniformly across the blade without interveinal amber bacterial crusts.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Streaks Coalescence & Canopy Browning**: As the disease progresses, numerous fine streaks expand longitudinally and laterally, coalescing into large, irregular brownish-grey patches.
- **Greyish Blanched Canopy**: In advanced severe attacks, the entire upper leaf canopy turns greyish-brown to straw-colored, resembling severe blast or blight from a distance.
- **Glume Infection**: In heavy infestations, bacteria invade the glumes and floral parts, causing brownish necrotic spots on developing spikelets and grain discoloration.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Infection Pathway**: Unlike BLB which penetrates via hydathodes into vascular bundles, the BLS pathogen enters primarily through open stomata or mechanical abrasion wounds into the parenchymatous mesophyll tissue.
- **Survival**: Survives on infected seed surfaces, dry rice straw, volunteer seedlings, and wild rice relatives (*Oryza rufipogon*).
- **Epidemiological Drivers**: High temperatures ($28\text{–}32^\circ\text{C}$), high relative humidity ($>85\%$), overcast rainy days, and heavy rainstorms that create leaf micro-wounds accelerate epidemic spread.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
- **Tillering Phase**: Initial infection establishes on lower leaves; spreads rapidly across adjacent hills.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Critical phase where rapid upward spread reaches middle canopy and flag leaves.
- **Heading Stage**: Flag leaf destruction impairs grain filling; glume infection causes grain spotting.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Standard insect-like ETLs are not applicable to foliar bacterial pathogens).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: Initiate preventive management when **$1\text{–}5\%$ of leaf area exhibits interveinal translucent streaks** during the active tillering or booting stages in humid, stormy weather.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Isolated fine streaks confined between veins on $<5\%$ of lower leaves; no canopy browning.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Streaks turning yellowish-brown on $6\text{–}15\%$ of leaf area; visible yellow exudate beads.
  - *Severe / Spreading*: Coalesced brownish patches on $>30\%$ of leaf area; extensive upper canopy blighting.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for BLS)*:
    - SES 1–3: $1\%\text{–}5\%$ leaf area with narrow interveinal streaks (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}15\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $16\%\text{–}30\%$ leaf area affected with coalescing patches (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>30\%$ leaf area affected with extensive foliar necrosis (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Avoid Mechanical Injury**: Avoid rough handling or clipping of seedling tips during transplanting to minimize entry portals.
- **Balanced Fertilization**: Apply nitrogen in split doses; avoid heavy top-dressing of urea during cloudy, rainy weather. Ensure adequate potassium nutrition.
- **Field Sanitation**: Plow under infected stubble immediately after harvest; eradicate collateral grassy weed hosts along field bunds.
- **Clean Seed Usage**: Use certified disease-free seeds; do not save seeds from heavily blighted seed crops.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Pseudomonas fluorescens**: Treat seeds @ 10 g/kg seed and apply foliar spray @ 2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water at the first sign of interveinal streaking.
- **Plant Extract Sprays**: Spray neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) or 20% fresh cow dung extract as preventive bio-bactericidal barriers.

## 10. Chemical Management
When bacterial leaf streak lesions begin spreading during tillering or booting, apply approved contact copper bactericides in 500 L water/ha:
- **Copper Hydroxide 77 WP** @ 1.25 kg/ha (2.5 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). CIBRC approved contact bactericide/fungicide; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 15 days.
- **Copper Oxychloride 50 WP** @ 1.25 kg/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Effective contact copper bactericide.
- **Antibiotic Notice**: Broad-spectrum agricultural antibiotics (e.g. Streptocycline) are `RESTRICTED` due to AMR concerns; contact copper bactericides represent the standard verified regulatory treatment.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Transmitted Light Scouting**: Hold leaves against the morning sun; look for bright, translucent yellow streaks between veins.
- **Scout Post-Storm**: Conduct field scouting 2 to 4 days after heavy rainfall accompanied by strong winds, as storm damage greatly accelerates BLS outbreaks.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agricultural Extension Officer or KVK Plant Pathologist if:
- **Rapid Canopy Bleaching**: More than 30% of flag leaf area becomes necrotic before flowering.
- **Diagnostic Confusion with BLB**: Uncertainty between BLS and BLB to ensure proper varietal resistance and containment strategies.
