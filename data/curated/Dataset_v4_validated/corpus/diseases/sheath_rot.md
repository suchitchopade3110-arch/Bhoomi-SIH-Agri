# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Sheath Rot

## 1. Identification
Sheath rot, caused by the fungal pathogen *Sarocladium oryzae* (formerly *Acrocylindrium oryzae*), is a damaging panicle-choking disease of rice that attacks the uppermost flag leaf sheath enclosing the young developing panicle during the booting and heading stages.

Farmers and field scouts can identify sheath rot by its characteristic flag leaf and panicle symptoms:
- **Flag Leaf Sheath Lesions**: Irregular, oblong or oblong-elliptical spots (0.5 to 1.5 cm long) appear on the **outermost flag leaf sheath** that covers the emerging panicle.
- **Lesion Morphology**: Lesions possess greyish-white or light brown centers surrounded by distinct, irregular dark reddish-brown to chocolate-brown margins.
- **Powdery White Fungal Mycelium**: When the infected boot leaf sheath is peeled open, a delicate, white powdery mass of fungal mycelium and conidia is visible coating the inner sheath wall and the trapped panicle.
- **Boot Choking (கதிர் வெளிவராமல் அடைபடுதல்)**: The young panicle is unable to emerge fully from the rotted boot sheath, remaining partially or completely trapped (*choked*).

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate field differential diagnosis is vital:
- **Sheath Rot vs. Sheath Blight (*Rhizoctonia solani*)**: Sheath rot attacks strictly the **uppermost flag leaf sheath enclosing the young panicle during booting**, forming oblong greyish lesions with powdery white mycelium and causing panicle choking. Sheath blight initiates on **lower leaf sheaths near the waterline during tillering**, forming broad snake-skin lesions with brown mustard-like sclerotia that climb upward.
- **Sheath Rot vs. Neck Blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae*)**: Sheath rot causes extensive rotting of the flag leaf sheath tissue and prevents panicle exertion with white powdery mycelium inside. Neck blast attacks strictly the neck node of an already emerged panicle, causing a black girdle that snaps without rotting the boot sheath.
- **Sheath Rot vs. Stem Borer Damage**: Stem borer larvae sever the stem culm internally causing loose white ears, whereas sheath rot glues the unexerted panicle inside the rotted sheath with visible fungal growth.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Incomplete Panicle Emergence & Grain Rotting**: Panicles either fail to emerge entirely or emerge only partially. Grains inside the choked panicle turn dark reddish-brown to blackish-brown and rot into sterile, unfilled chaff.
- **Secondary Grain Discoloration**: Infected spikelets become colonized by secondary molds, leading to severe grain discoloration and dirty panicle syndrome.
- **Association with Mites and Insects**: Sheath rot infection is strongly aggravated by feeding punctures caused by the rice sheath mite (*Steneotarsonemus spinki*) or yellow stem borer larvae entering the boot.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Infection Mechanism**: The pathogen is an opportunistic wound invader that enters through stomata, insect feeding punctures, or micro-cracks in the flag leaf sheath during panicle expansion.
- **Survival**: Survives on infected seed surfaces, crop stubble, and collateral hosts (*Cyperus*, *Echinochloa*).
- **Epidemiological Triggers**: High relative humidity ($>85\%$), warm temperatures ($25\text{–}30^\circ\text{C}$), rain showers during panicle emergence, excess nitrogen fertilization, and high sheath mite populations.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Sheath rot attacks rice strictly during the reproductive booting and heading window:
- **Vegetative Phase (Seedling to Tillering)**: Not susceptible.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage (Critical Vulnerability Zone)**: Peak phase where pathogen penetrates the boot leaf sheath enclosing the young inflorescence.
- **Heading / Panicle Emergence Stage**: Culmination of damage; boot choking and grain discoloration.
- **Grain Maturation**: Grains on partially emerged panicles remain discolored and sterile.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Standard numerical ETLs are not established for sheath rot).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: Apply preventive fungicidal treatment at the **50% boot leaf stage (prior to flowering)** in fields with a history of sheath rot or when $\ge 5\%\text{ of tillers}$ show oblong grey lesions on the flag leaf sheath.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Small oblong grey lesions on $<5\%$ of flag leaf sheaths; panicles emerging normally.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Lesions enlarging on $6\text{–}15\%$ of tillers with grey-white powdery growth inside sheath.
  - *Severe / Spreading*: $>16\text{–}30\%$ of tillers showing choked, partially emerged panicles with brown grain rot.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>30\%$ of tillers with completely rotted, unexerted panicles; massive yield collapse.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for Sheath Rot)*:
    - SES 1–3: $<5\%$ tillers showing oblong grey lesions on flag leaf sheath (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}15\%$ tillers affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $16\%\text{–}30\%$ tillers with choked/partially emerged panicles (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>30\%$ tillers with completely rotted choked panicles (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Balanced Nitrogen Nutrition**: Avoid excess urea top-dressing during the booting stage. Apply nitrogen in balanced splits with adequate potassium.
- **Wider Plant Spacing**: Maintain optimum spacing ($20\text{ cm} \times 15\text{ cm}$) to reduce canopy microclimatic humidity during booting.
- **Mite Management**: Control sheath mites (*Steneotarsonemus spinki*) using acaricides (e.g. Dicofol / Spiromesifen) where mite populations are high.
- **Field Sanitation**: Remove and burn infected crop residues and wild grasses from field bunds.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Seed Treatment with Biocontrol Agents**: Treat seeds with *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 10 g/kg seed to eliminate seed-borne conidia.
- **Foliar Biocontrol**: Spray *Pseudomonas fluorescens* @ 2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water at boot leaf stage to colonize flag leaf sheaths preventively.

## 10. Chemical Management
When lesions appear on flag leaf sheaths or as a preventive spray at the **50% boot leaf stage**, apply approved systemic fungicides in 500 L water/ha:
- **Carbendazim 50 WP** @ 500 g/ha (1 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Broad-spectrum systemic benzimidazole fungicide; spray at boot leaf and panicle emergence stages; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days.
- **Propiconazole 25 EC** @ 500 ml/ha (1 ml/L water) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Systemic triazole fungicide providing dual control of sheath rot and false smut; PHI: 30 days.
- **Hexaconazole 5 SC** @ 1000 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Systemic triazole fungicide.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Boot Leaf Inspection**: Carefully examine the flag leaf sheaths of 20 hills per acre during the booting stage for oblong greyish-brown lesions.
- **Sheath Peeling Test**: Peel open symptomatic flag leaf sheaths to verify the presence of white powdery *Sarocladium* mycelium and assess panicle emergence status.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Plant Pathologist if:
- **High Percentage of Boot Choking**: More than 20% of panicles fail to emerge across commercial fields.
- **Co-Infestation with Sheath Mites**: Severe sheath rotting accompanied by high sheath mite populations (requires integrated fungicide-acaricide tank mix).
