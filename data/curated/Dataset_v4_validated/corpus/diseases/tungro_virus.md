# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Rice Tungro Disease (RTV)

## 1. Identification
Rice Tungro Disease (RTV) is a severe, epidemic-prone viral disease of rice across South and Southeast Asia, caused by a composite infection of two distinct plant viruses:
1. **Rice Tungro Bacilliform Virus (RTBV)**: A double-stranded DNA badnavirus responsible for the characteristic disease symptoms (stunting and yellowing).
2. **Rice Tungro Spherical Virus (RTSV)**: A single-stranded RNA waikavirus required as an essential helper virus for insect vector acquisition and transmission.

Farmers and field scouts can identify Tungro disease by its striking foliar and habitus symptoms:
- **Severe Plant Stunting (பயிர் குட்டையாதல்)**: Infested hills stop elongating, displaying extreme dwarfing and markedly reduced tiller production.
- **Vivid Yellow-Orange Leaf Discoloration**: Foliage turns bright yellow, yellow-orange, or deep rusty orange, initiating at the leaf tips and extending downwards along the leaf margins.
- **Interveinal Mottling & Rust Specks**: Youngest expanding leaves exhibit faint mottled patterns, interveinal chlorosis, and small rusty necrotic spots.
- **Delayed Flowering & Chaffy Panicles**: Infected plants flower very late or fail to exert panicles; emerged panicles are small, sterile, and bear dark brown blotched grains.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate differential diagnosis is vital to prevent confusing viral Tungro with nutritional chlorosis or drought:
- **Tungro vs. Nutritional Nitrogen / Iron Deficiency**: In nitrogen/iron deficiency, chlorosis occurs uniformly across entire fields or soil blocks without severe hill stunting, and roots remain white and healthy. Tungro occurs in **scattered patchy foci (hotspots)** with **marked stunting**, rusty orange discoloration, and stunted dark brown root systems.
- **Tungro vs. Zinc Deficiency (Khaira Disease)**: Zinc deficiency causes rusty-brown pigmentation that initiates on older lower leaves 2 to 3 weeks after transplanting, which recovers upon zinc sulphate application. Tungro causes vivid orange-yellowing that affects both young and old leaves, accompanied by viral phloem necrosis.
- **Diagnostic Iodine Starch Test (இலை அயோடின் பரிசோதனை)**: Cut a 5 cm section of symptomatic leaf blade in early morning. Immerse in 10% alcohol to decolorize chlorophyll, then place in 2% Iodine-Potassium Iodide ($I_2\text{-}KI$) solution for 5 minutes. **Tungro-infected leaves turn deep blue-black** due to abnormal starch accumulation in virus-blocked phloem, whereas non-viral nutritional yellowing remains light brown.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Vector-Driven Patchy Spread**: The disease spreads outward from initial infection foci following the flight paths of the Green Leafhopper (*Nephotettix virescens*).
- **Root Degeneration**: Infested plants exhibit a poorly developed, stunted root system with blackish-brown rootlets, causing the plant to pull out easily from the soil.
- **Total Crop Failure in Young Seedlings**: Infection occurring in nursery or early tillering stages results in 100% loss of grain yield.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Insect Vector Transmission**: RTV is transmitted in a **semi-persistent manner** exclusively by leafhoppers, predominantly the **Green Leafhopper (*Nephotettix virescens*)**, and to a lesser extent *Nephotettix nigropictus*.
- **Vector Acquisition Dynamics**: Both nymphs and adults acquire the virus complex within 15 to 30 minutes of feeding on an infected plant, and can inoculate healthy rice plants within 10 to 15 minutes. The vector retains infectivity for 5 to 7 days, but the virus is not passed to insect eggs.
- **Reservoir Hosts**: Overwinters and survives in volunteer rice seedlings, ratoon rice crops, and wild grassy weeds (*Leersia hexandra*, *Echinochloa colona*, *Cyperus rotundus*).

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Plant vulnerability decreases sharply with crop age:
- **Nursery / Seedling Stage (1 to 20 Days After Sowing)**: **Extreme critical danger zone**; early infection induces total stunting and complete crop loss.
- **Early Vegetative Stage (1 to 30 DAT)**: Highly susceptible; severe stunting and catastrophic yield reduction.
- **Active Tillering Stage (30 to 45 DAT)**: Moderate susceptibility; yield reduction proportional to infected tillers.
- **Booting, Heading & Maturity (Beyond 45 DAT)**: Plants become resistant; late infection causes minor foliar yellowing with negligible yield loss.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Tungro has no direct ETL because the virus cannot be cured post-infection; management relies strictly on **vector GLH thresholds**).
- **Vector Action Trigger (Green Leafhopper Thresholds)**:
  - *Nursery Stage in Tungro Tracts*: $\ge 1\text{ to }2\text{ GLH per hill}$.
  - *Vegetative Stage in Tungro Tracts*: $\ge 2\text{ GLH per hill}$ (or $\ge 1\text{ GLH/sweep}$ with active Tungro in vicinity).

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Slight stunting and faint yellowing on youngest leaf tips in $<5\%$ of hills in isolated patches.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Conspicuous yellow-orange discoloration and reduced tillering in $6\text{–}15\%$ of hills.
  - *Severe / Spreading*: $>16\text{–}30\%$ of hills showing severe stunting, rusty orange foliage, and delayed flowering.
  - *Catastrophic / Epidemic*: Field-wide epidemic with $>30\%$ stunted orange hills; complete crop failure.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for Rice Tungro)*:
    - SES 1–3: $<5\%$ infected hills in isolated patches (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}15\%$ infected hills with marked stunting (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $16\%\text{–}30\%$ infected hills with delayed flowering (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>30\%$ infected hills with complete field-wide collapse (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant Varieties (Primary Defense)**: Plant Tungro-resistant and GLH-resistant cultivars suited for South India, such as CO 51, ADT 45, ADT 43, ASD 16, and IR 36.
- **Roguing & Destroying Infected Hills**: Promptly uproot and bury stunted yellow-orange hills during the first 4 weeks after transplanting to eliminate the virus inoculum source.
- **Synchronous Planting**: Practice synchronous planting across village holdings to avoid staggered cropping that maintains continuous green leafhopper populations.
- **Plow Under Ratoons & Stubble**: Immediately plow and flood fields after harvest to destroy ratoon rice and volunteer seedlings that harbor the virus.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Conserve Natural GLH Predators**: Protect predatory spiders (*Lycosa pseudoannulata*, *Oxyopes javanus*, *Atypena formosana*) and mirid bugs (*Cyrtorhinus lividipennis*), which keep leafhopper vector populations below viral transmission thresholds.
- **Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE 5%)**: Spray NSKE 5% in nursery beds as a natural feeding deterrent against leafhoppers.

## 10. Chemical Management (Targeting Vector GLH)
**Crucial Medical Rule**: There is **no chemical cure** for the plant once infected with Tungro virus. Chemical management must focus exclusively on **suppressing the insect vector (*Nephotettix virescens*)** in the nursery and early tillering stages before virus transmission occurs:
- **Imidacloprid 17.8 SL** @ 100–125 ml/ha (20–25 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly systemic neonicotinoid providing rapid knockdown of GLH vectors; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 21 days.
- **Thiamethoxam 25 WG** @ 100 g/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Systemic translaminar foliar spray; PHI: 14 days.
- **Buprofezin 25 SC** @ 400–800 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Insect Growth Regulator (IGR) for hopper nymph suppression.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Perform Iodine Starch Test**: Verify suspected viral yellowing using the $I_2\text{-}KI$ field starch test before prescribing vector sprays.
- **Sweep Net Vector Scouting**: Sweep 10 times across nursery and vegetative plots to monitor Green Leafhopper vector densities.
- **Light Trap Catches**: Monitor light traps for adult leafhopper surges indicating migratory flight waves.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Plant Virologist if:
- **Rapid Outbreak Across Adjacent Villages**: More than 15% of hills show bright orange discoloration and severe stunting across multiple contiguous holdings.
- **High GLH Vector Density in Nursery**: Over 5 hoppers per seedling in nursery beds during regional Tungro warnings.
