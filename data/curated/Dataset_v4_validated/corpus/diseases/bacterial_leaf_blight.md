# TNAU & ICAR Rice Disease Management Guide: Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB)

## 1. Identification
Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB), caused by the vascular rod-shaped bacterium *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae*, is one of the most destructive bacterial diseases of paddy across Tamil Nadu and tropical Asia.

Farmers and field scouts can identify the disease by its signature two-phase symptom complex:
- **Kresek Phase (Early Systemic Wilt)**: Occurs in young seedlings 1 to 3 weeks after transplanting. Leaves roll inward, turn dull greyish-green, wilt rapidly, and entire tillers or hills collapse and die.
- **Leaf Blight Phase (Foliar Phase)**: Manifests from tillering to heading. Small water-soaked translucent stripes initiate at the leaf tip or margins, rapidly expanding into long, pale yellow to straw-colored lesions with characteristic **wavy or undulated margins**.
- **Bacterial Ooze**: On warm, humid mornings with heavy dew, tiny milky-white to amber-colored bacterial exudate beads (*ooze*) emerge on the surface of young lesions, later drying into hard, yellowish crusts.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Accurate differential diagnosis is vital to prevent misapplication of synthetic fungicides:
- **BLB vs. Bacterial Leaf Streak (BLS)**: BLB produces broad marginal lesions with distinctly **wavy margins** that progress down the leaf blade and enter the vascular system. In contrast, BLS (*Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzicola*) forms narrow, fine, rectangular **interveinal streaks** strictly delimited between leaf veins.
- **BLB vs. Nitrogen / Drought Scorching**: BLB lesions display a characteristic wavy boundary separating dead yellow-straw tissue from healthy green tissue, and exhibit bacterial streaming during the ooze test. Drought and fertilizer scorch affect leaf tips uniformly across the field without wavy margins or bacterial ooze.
- **Kresek vs. Stem Borer Dead Heart**: Kresek wilt causes the entire clump of leaves in a tiller to wilt and roll together; pulling the tiller resists easy detachment and exhibits rotting at the submerged collar without chewed caterpillar frass. Stem borer dead hearts slide out effortlessly with chewed frass at the severed base.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Marginal Foliar Blighting**: Lesions enlarge along one or both leaf margins, coalescing to cover large portions of the leaf blade, eventually killing the flag leaf.
- **Canopy Blighting & Lodging**: Severely diseased fields exhibit a bleached, whitish-straw scorched appearance. Panicles on blighted tillers remain poorly exerted with high grain chaffiness.
- **Diagnostic Ooze Test (Field Test)**: Cut an active lesion margin (2 cm piece), place it in a transparent glass of clear water against light; within 2 to 5 minutes, cloudy, milky-white bacterial streaming (*ooze*) flows out from the severed vascular bundles.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Infection Pathway**: Bacteria enter through hydathodes on leaf tips/margins, stomata, or mechanical root/foliar wounds sustained during transplanting or storm winds.
- **Systemic Vascular Colonization**: Bacteria multiply rapidly within the xylem vessels, producing extracellular polysaccharides (EPS) that clog water transport, causing desiccation and wilting.
- **Survival & Inoculum Reservoirs**: Survives in infected rice stubble, volunteer rice seedlings, contaminated seed hulls, and collateral weed hosts (*Leersia hexandra*, *Cyperus rotundus*).
- **Epidemiological Triggers**: Favored by high relative humidity ($>85\%$), warm temperatures ($25\text{–}34^\circ\text{C}$), heavy monsoon rains with gusty winds, and excess nitrogenous fertilization.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
- **Nursery / Early Transplanting (1 to 25 DAT)**: Peak danger zone for catastrophic systemic *Kresek* wilting.
- **Maximum Tillering to Panicle Initiation**: Leaf blight phase initiates; rapid lateral spread during rainy weather.
- **Booting to Heading Stage**: Flag leaf blighting directly curtails grain filling, inducing up to 50% yield loss in susceptible varieties.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) / Decision Threshold
- **ETL Status**: `ETL_STATUS: NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Conventional insect-like numerical ETLs are not applicable to systemic bacterial vascular diseases).
- **Evidence-Supported Action Trigger**: Initiate preventive and bactericidal interventions immediately upon detecting the **first appearance of water-soaked marginal streaks or 1–5% diseased leaf area** during tillering in disease-prone weather.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Low Severity*: Isolated leaf tip yellowing with wavy margins on $<5\%$ of leaves in lower canopy.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Lesions expanding along margins on $6\text{–}12\%$ of leaves; visible bacterial beads on damp mornings.
  - *Severe / Epidemic*: Lesions coalescing across $>25\%$ of leaf area; flag leaf blighting or active Kresek wilting of hills.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for BLB)*:
    - SES 1–3: $1\%\text{–}5\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5: $6\%\text{–}12\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7: $13\%\text{–}25\%$ leaf area affected (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9: $>25\%\text{–}50\%$ leaf area affected or severe Kresek phase (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant Cultivars (Primary Defense)**: Plant resistant varieties carrying *Xa* resistance genes, such as CO 51, ADT 43, TNAU Rice TRY 3, and Improved Samba Mahsuri.
- **Nutrient Balancing**: Split nitrogen into 3 equal doses; avoid top-dressing urea during active blight spread. Apply extra potash (muriate of potash @ 25 kg/ha) to strengthen cell walls.
- **Water Management**: Drain field water for 2 to 3 days during early disease outbreak; avoid flowing irrigation water from infected to healthy fields.
- **Avoid Seedling Clipping**: Strictly prohibit clipping seedling leaf tips during transplanting, which creates open entry wounds for bacteria.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Fresh Cow Dung Extract (20%)**: Spray 20% fresh cow dung extract (20 kg fresh cow dung dissolved in 100 L water, filtered, and sprayed twice at 15-day intervals), a validated TNAU non-chemical recommendation providing antagonistic phyllosphere microflora.
- **Pseudomonas fluorescens**: Treat seeds @ 10 g/kg and apply foliar spray @ 2.5 kg/ha mixed in 500 L water at early tillering.

## 10. Chemical Management
Apply approved bactericides at early symptom onset in 500 L water/ha:
- **Copper Hydroxide 77 WP** @ 1.25 kg/ha (2.5 g/L water) (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). CIBRC approved contact copper bactericide; creates protective barrier on leaf surfaces; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 15 days.
- **Copper Oxychloride 50 WP** @ 1.25 kg/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Broad-spectrum contact bactericide.
- **Streptocycline (Streptomycin Sulphate 90% + Tetracycline Hydrochloride 10%)** @ 100 g/ha mixed with Copper Oxychloride 500 g/ha (`RESTRICTED` / `AMR_WARNING`).
  - *Antibiotic Regulatory Warning*: Streptocycline is an agricultural antibiotic formulation subject to severe regulatory scrutiny, phased restrictions, and bans under Central Insecticides Board (CIBRC) / DPPQS directives due to Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) risks in human and veterinary health. Current ICAR/TNAU guidelines prioritize non-antibiotic copper compounds (Copper Hydroxide) and resistant varieties.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Morning Scouting**: Inspect leaf margins across 20 hills per acre during early morning hours (6:30 to 8:30 AM) when dew highlights bacterial exudate droplets.
- **Field Margin Inspection**: Pay special attention to field borders near tree shade or water inlets where humidity is highest.
- **Perform Ooze Test**: Confirm bacterial etiology before prescribing chemical sprays.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Plant Pathologist if:
- **Rapid Kresek Wilt Spread**: More than 10% of transplanted hills collapse within 10 days of transplanting.
- **Breakdown of Varietal Resistance**: Severe foliar blighting observed in known resistant varieties (indicates emergence of new virulent *Xanthomonas* pathotypes).
- **Ineffectiveness of Copper Sprays**: Disease progression continues unchecked after two copper bactericide sprays.
