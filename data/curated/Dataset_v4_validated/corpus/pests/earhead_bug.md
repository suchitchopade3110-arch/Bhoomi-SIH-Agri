# ICAR Rice Insect Pest Management: Earhead Bug

## 1. Identification
The rice earhead bug or gundhi bug (*Leptocorisa acuta*) is a specialized grain-feeding hemipteran pest of paddy across India, infesting crops strictly during the flowering, milking, and soft-dough stages of panicle development.

Farmers and scouts can identify the bug in the field through its distinctive morphology and odor:
- **Adult Bug**: Slender, elongated, olive-green to brownish-green bug (15 to 20 mm long) with long, slender legs, 4-segmented antennae, and a prominent piercing-sucking proboscis. When handled or disturbed, both adults and nymphs emit a strong, pungent, defensive foul odor ("gundhi"), giving the pest its common name.
- **Nymphs**: Wingless, pale green nymphs with long legs and dark-banded antennae, moving actively among the panicle spikelets.
- **Eggs**: Disc-shaped, dark reddish-brown to blackish eggs laid in neat linear rows of 10 to 20 on the upper leaf surface along the midrib.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Earhead bug infestation can be clearly distinguished from fungal grain discoloration and stem borer white earheads by the following field cues:
- **Punctured Grains with Brown Spots**: The bug inserts its stylet into the developing grain to suck the milk. Each feeding puncture produces a tiny brown or black puncture spot on the glume, often accompanied by an exuded drop of dried starch.
- **Partially Filled & Chaffy Panicles**: Panicles infested during early milking contain many shriveled, partially filled, or empty discolored grains, but the panicle stem itself remains green and attached to the plant, unlike stem borer where the entire earhead is completely severed and white.
- **Characteristic Foul Odor**: A distinct offensive bedbug-like odor pervades infested fields during early morning and late evening hours when bug activity peaks.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Grain Milking Depletion**: Nymphs and adults suck the liquid endosperm from developing grains during the milking phase, leaving grains empty (chaffy) or shriveled and ill-filled.
- **Pecky Rice & Grain Chalkiness**: Grains damaged at the soft dough stage develop internal chalky patches and black necrotic spots ("pecky rice"), which break easily during milling, severely downgrading commercial market value.
- **Secondary Fungal Invasions**: Feeding punctures allow secondary opportunistic fungi (*Curvularia*, *Fusarium*, *Helminthosporium*) to enter, causing severe grain discoloration and dirty panicle syndrome.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Oviposition**: A female lays 100 to 250 eggs in straight rows on leaf blades over 2 to 3 weeks. Incubation lasts 5 to 8 days.
- **Nymphal Instars**: Nymphs develop through 5 instars over 15 to 20 days, feeding voraciously on milky grains.
- **Adult Habits & Activity Windows**: Adults live for 30 to 50 days. Peak feeding and flight activity occurs during cool, humid hours (early morning 6:00 to 9:00 AM and late evening 4:30 to 7:00 PM). During hot midday hours, bugs hide in lower foliage and bund grasses.
- **Alternate Hosts**: Breeds on wild grasses (*Echinochloa*, *Panicum*, *Paspalum*, *Setaria*) before migrating to flowering rice crops.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Earhead bug attacks rice exclusively during panicle emergence and grain development:
- **Panicle Emergence & Flowering Stage**: Initial bug immigration; feeding on newly opened spikelets.
- **Milking Stage (Peak Vulnerability Window)**: Liquid milky grains are highly susceptible; massive feeding causes complete grain emptiness and yield loss.
- **Soft Dough Stage**: Grains become shriveled and discolored ("pecky rice").
- **Hard Dough & Maturity Stages**: Grains harden and are no longer susceptible to bug feeding.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Field chemical interventions must be strictly based on the following source-quoted action thresholds:
- **ETL Classification**: `NUMERIC_ETL`
- **Flowering Stage**: $\ge 5\text{ bugs per 100 panicles}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
- **Milking / Grain-Filling Stage**: $\ge 10\text{ bugs per 100 panicles}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Sampling Modifier*: Equivalent to $\mathbf{1\text{–}2\text{ bugs per hill}}$ in standard hill-based field sampling.
- *Distinction from Severity*: ETL identifies the precise bug density per panicle requiring immediate insecticide application. Severity evaluates the resultant grain chalkiness, pecky rice percentage, and market damage.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Early Severity*: Isolated adult bugs feeding during morning hours ($1\text{–}4\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$, $<1\text{ bug/hill}$); rare localized brownish puncture spots on glumes.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Milky grains being pierced ($5\text{–}10\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$, $1\text{–}2\text{ bugs/hill}$); foul odor noticeable upon entering field; brown spots on maturing grains.
  - *Severe Severity*: Numerous damaged, shriveled, partially filled grains ($11\text{–}25\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$, $2\text{–}4\text{ bugs/hill}$) with blackened feeding punctures; strong pungent odor.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>25\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$ ($>4\text{ bugs/hill}$); over 30% empty/chaffy erect panicles with massive economic loss.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *ICAR-IIRR Earhead Bug Damage Scale*:
    - SES 1–3 (Early): $1\text{–}4\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$ ($<1\text{ bug/hill}$) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5 (Moderate): $5\text{–}10\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$ ($1\text{–}2\text{ bugs/hill}$) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
    - SES 7 (Severe): $11\text{–}25\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$ ($2\text{–}4\text{ bugs/hill}$) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9 (Catastrophic): $>25\text{ bugs/100 panicles}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
  - *Threshold Status*: `SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT` (Hill-based sampling duality documented).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Synchronous Community Planting**: Synchronize sowing and transplanting across adjacent fields in a village tract so that all crops flower simultaneously, preventing pest concentration on early or late-flowering fields.
- **Bund Sanitation (Weed Host Removal)**: Trim and clean wild grassy weeds (*Echinochloa colona*, *Panicum repens*, *Paspalum*) from field bunds and water channels before panicle emergence to destroy alternate breeding sites.
- **Hand Sweeping & Netting**: Sweep panicles with hand nets in early morning hours (6:00 to 8:00 AM) when bugs are sluggish.
- **Light Traps**: Install light traps during flowering and milking stages to monitor and mass-trap nocturnal adult bugs.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Egg Parasitoids**: Conserve natural scelionid and encyrtid egg parasitoids (*Gryon* spp., *Ooencyrtus* spp.) which can parasitize over 60% of earhead bug eggs in unsprayed fields.
- **Predatory Spiders & Reduviid Bugs**: Protect long-jawed spiders (*Tetragnatha* spp.) and assassin bugs (*Sycanus* spp.) which actively prey on nymphs and adult bugs among panicles.

## 10. Chemical Management
When pest density exceeds the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) during the milking phase, apply approved insecticides strictly during early morning (before 8:30 AM) or late evening (after 4:30 PM) when bugs are active on panicles and pollinators are absent:
- **Malathion 50 EC** @ 500–1000 ml/ha (100–200 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`RESTRICTED`). *Regulatory & Residue Warning: Organophosphate insecticide applied directly to developing grain heads; carries high grain residue hazard. Mandatory minimum Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI) of 7 to 10 days before grain harvest must be strictly observed.*
- **Azadirachtin 1% (10,000 ppm)** @ 1000 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Eco-friendly botanical repellent with zero residue hazard; ideal for late milking sprays.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Panicle Counting Protocol**: Walk diagonally across the field and inspect 100 panicles (20 panicles in 5 distinct spots) during early morning (6:30 to 8:00 AM) or late afternoon.
- **Net Sweep Counts**: Conduct 20 sweeps with a standard insect net across the crop canopy.
- **Smell & Visual Clues**: Note the characteristic pungent odor in the field as an initial indicator of bug presence.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Entomologist if:
- **Widespread Grain Discoloration**: Over 25% of maturing grains exhibit black puncturing or dirty panicle syndrome across the holding.
- **Late Staggered Flowering Infestation**: Late-planted fields are heavily invaded by bugs migrating from harvested adjacent fields.
- **Residue Hazard Violation**: Ensure farmers do not spray persistent organophosphates within 7 days of harvest.
