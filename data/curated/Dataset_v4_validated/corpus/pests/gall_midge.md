# TNAU Gall Midge Management in Rice

## 1. Identification
The rice gall midge (*Orseolia oryzae*) is an internal tissue-feeding dipteran pest causing severe yield loss across Tamil Nadu, particularly in the Cauvery Delta, coastal belts, and low-lying waterlogged tracts.

Farmers and scouts can identify the pest through its life stages and characteristic symptoms:
- **Adult Midge**: Small, delicate, nocturnal, mosquito-like fly (3 to 4 mm long). The female has a bright orange-red abdomen and long, slender legs and antennae, while the male is darker and slightly smaller.
- **Egg**: Elongated, tubular, reddish or pinkish eggs laid singly or in clusters of 3 to 5 on the ligules or undersurface of leaf sheaths.
- **Maggot (Larva)**: Pale, translucent, legless maggot (3 to 4 mm long) with a distinct pointed head, which bores into the growing tip.
- **Pupa**: Pale pinkish pupa enclosed within the tubular gall; it wriggles upward to the tip of the gall tube just before adult emergence.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Gall midge damage produces unique morphological distortions that cannot be confused with other paddy pests when carefully examined:
- **Silver Shoot / Onion Leaf (வெள்ளிக்குருத்து)**: The central growing point of the infested tiller is modified into a hollow, tubular, pale-green to silvery-white elongation resembling an onion leaf or elephant tusk ("Aanaikomban").
- **Tiller Sterility**: Unlike stem borer "dead hearts" which pull out easily and show chewed frass at the base, gall midge silver shoots are firmly attached, completely sterile, and incapable of ever developing a panicle.
- **Pupal Exuviae at Tube Apex**: Before the adult emerges, the pupa wriggles to the apex of the silver tube, leaving behind an empty, brownish-translucent pupal case protruding from the tip.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Apical Bud Galling**: The maggot crawls down between the leaf sheaths to the apical growing point (apical meristem). Larval secretions stimulate abnormal cellular hypertrophy, transforming the leaf sheath into a silvery tube.
- **Profuse Stunting & Bushing**: Infestation of primary tillers induces the plant to produce numerous weak, stunted secondary and tertiary tillers that remain vegetative and unproductive ("bushing effect").
- **Total Loss of Panicle Formation**: Every tiller displaying a silver shoot fails to produce a grain-bearing panicle, causing direct yield loss proportional to the percentage of silver shoots.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Oviposition**: Female midges lay 100 to 200 eggs near the ligules during humid night hours. Incubation lasts 3 to 4 days.
- **Larval Period**: The maggot feeds inside the growing apical bud for 8 to 12 days through 3 to 4 instars, initiating gall formation.
- **Pupation**: Pupal stage lasts 3 to 5 days inside the gall tube.
- **Favorable Factors**: High atmospheric humidity ($>85\%$), continuous cloudiness, cloudy rainy spells during tillering, and delayed planting greatly favor severe gall midge epidemics.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Gall midge attacks rice exclusively during the early vegetative development window:
- **Seedling / Nursery Stage**: Eggs laid on young seedlings; early maggot penetration.
- **Active Tillering Stage (15 to 45 Days After Transplanting)**: Peak vulnerability period where primary tillers are transformed into silver shoots.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Once panicle initiation occurs (beyond 45 DAT), the growing point is no longer susceptible to gall midge galling.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Field chemical interventions must be strictly initiated when pest scouting confirms that gall midge populations have reached or exceeded the following source-quoted threshold:
- **Vegetative (Tillering) Stage**: $\ge 5\%\text{ silver shoots}$ OR $\ge 1\text{ adult midge per 100 net sweeps}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **Low / Sub-ETL ($<2\%\text{ silver shoots}$)**: Trace incidence; natural parasitoids active; monitor twice weekly; avoid synthetic chemical spraying.
- **Moderate / At-ETL ($2\text{–}5\%\text{ silver shoots}$)**: Threshold approaching; prepare biological releases or soil-applied systemic controls.
- **High / Severe ($>5\%\text{ silver shoots}$)**: Critical threshold breached; apply systemic granular/foliar treatment immediately before tillering phase closes.

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant Cultivars (Primary Defense)**: Plant gall midge resistant paddy varieties suited for South India, such as Kavya, Surekha, Shakthi, Vikram, and IR 36.
- **Early & Synchronous Planting**: Complete transplanting before the onset of heavy monsoon rains (before late August in Kharif / Kuruvai) to avoid peak midge emergence.
- **Deep Summer Plowing**: Plow field bunds and stubble immediately after harvest to expose overwintering pupae to intense solar heat and predatory birds.
- **Light Trap Deployment**: Install light traps @ 1 per hectare to attract and kill nocturnal adult midges.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Larval Parasitoids**: Conserve the highly effective natural hymenopteran parasitoid *Platygaster oryzae* (*Platygaster orseoliae*), which can parasitize up to 80% of gall midge larvae in untreated fields.
- **Predatory Spiders & Carabids**: Protect predatory spiders (*Lycosa*, *Tetragnatha*) and ground beetles that consume adult midges during nighttime emergence.

## 10. Chemical Management
When gall midge density exceeds the Economic Threshold Level (ETL) during the active tillering window (15–30 DAT), apply validated systemic treatments:
- **Thiamethoxam 25 WG** @ 100 g/ha (40 g/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Translaminar systemic insecticide; safe to natural predators; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 14 days.
- **Fipronil 5 SC** @ 1000–1500 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Systemic phenylpyrazole; suppresses internal maggots; PHI: 32 days.
- **Carbofuran 3G** @ 33 kg/ha at planting or early tillering (`RESTRICTED`). *Regulatory Warning: Class Ib high-toxicity carbamate restricted due to extreme groundwater and aquatic toxicity; non-chemical resistant varieties and safer systemic alternatives strongly preferred.*

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Silver Shoot Counting**: Count total tillers and number of silver shoots across 20 fixed hills per acre once every 4 days between 15 and 45 DAT.
- **Sweep Net Sampling**: Sweep 100 times across the field in early morning hours to estimate adult midge density.
- **Light Trap Catches**: Daily monitoring of light trap catches; catches exceeding $\ge 10\text{ midges/night}$ indicate an emerging brood.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Agronomist if:
- **Severe Silver Shoot Outbreak**: Over 10% silver shoots observed in resistant cultivars (indicates emergence of a new virulent Gall Midge Biotype).
- **Post-45 DAT Ineffectiveness**: Chemical applications attempted after panicle initiation should be blocked, as the plant is no longer susceptible and spray is economically wasteful.
- **Zero Parasitization**: Dissection of silver shoots reveals complete absence of *Platygaster oryzae* parasitoids in the field.
