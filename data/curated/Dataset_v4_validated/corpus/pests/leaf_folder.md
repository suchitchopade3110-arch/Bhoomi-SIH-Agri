# TNAU Rice Pest Management Guide: Leaf Folder

## 1. Identification
The rice leaf folder (*Cnaphalocrocis medinalis*) is a major lepidopteran defoliating pest of paddy throughout Tamil Nadu, thriving in shaded fields with lush vegetative growth and heavy nitrogenous fertilization.

Farmers and field scouts can identify the pest across its development stages:
- **Adult Moth**: Small, delicate, brownish-yellow to golden-yellow moth (wingspan 15 to 18 mm) with distinct dark brown, wavy marginal borders on both pairs of wings and two prominent dark wavy lines across the forewings.
- **Egg**: Shiny, flat, oval, scale-like whitish eggs laid singly or in small linear pairs on the undersurface of tender leaf blades near the midrib.
- **Larva (Caterpillar)**: Slender, active, yellowish-green caterpillar (15 to 20 mm long) with a brownish head. It wriggles backwards rapidly when touched or disturbed inside the leaf fold.
- **Pupa**: Reddish-brown pupa enclosed in a delicate silken cocoon inside the folded leaf.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Leaf folder feeding damage can be easily distinguished from other foliage disorders through specific visual traits:
- **Longitudinal Silken Folds**: Leaves are folded lengthwise along the margins and stitched together with fine white silken threads produced by the larva. The caterpillar resides and feeds entirely inside this tubular fold.
- **Transparent Papery Streaks**: Damaged leaves display distinct, longitudinal white or papery transparent streaks where the caterpillar has scraped the green chlorophyllous mesophyll tissue, leaving only the outer epidermal membrane intact.
- **Look-alike Distinction**: Whorl maggot damage creates ragged, serrated leaf margins at unfolding, whereas leaf folder maintains intact margins held together by silken threads.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Foliage Scraping & Scorching**: The internal scraping of leaf chlorophyll severely reduces photosynthetic capacity. In severe infestations, damaged leaves dry up, and the field exhibits a conspicuous whitish, scorched appearance from a distance.
- **Flag Leaf Damage (Critical Yield Hazard)**: Infestation during booting and flag leaf emergence compromises the flag leaf, which provides over 60% of the carbohydrates required for grain filling, leading to high grain chaffiness and yield reduction.
- **Silken Webs & Frass**: Inside the rolled leaves, dark granular larval excreta and empty pupal cases are readily observed.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Oviposition**: Female moths lay 100 to 150 eggs on leaf surfaces over 3 to 4 nights. Incubation period lasts 4 to 7 days.
- **Larval Development**: The larva undergoes 5 instars over 15 to 25 days. A single caterpillar can damage and roll 4 to 6 leaves during its larval lifespan.
- **Pupation**: Pupates within the rolled leaf for 6 to 8 days before the adult moth emerges during early evening hours.
- **Favorable Factors**: High relative humidity ($>80\%$), cloudy overcast weather, dense canopy shading, and excess nitrogen fertilization accelerate reproduction and population outbreaks.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Leaf folder attacks rice from the vegetative phase through flowering:
- **Tillering Phase**: Early foliar scraping and leaf rolling across tillers.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Critical vulnerability window; flag leaf protection is essential.
- **Flowering Stage**: Continued leaf damage reduces photosynthate translocation to developing grains.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Field chemical interventions must be strictly initiated when pest scouting confirms that leaf folder populations have reached or exceeded the following source-quoted thresholds:
- **Vegetative Stage**: $\ge 1\text{ larva per hill}$ OR $\ge 10\%\text{ damaged/folded leaves}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
- **Reproductive Stage**: $\ge 2\text{ larvae per hill}$ OR $\ge 20\%\text{ damaged/folded leaves}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Booting / Flag Leaf Modifier*: During the flag leaf emergence and booting stage, the action threshold drops to $\mathbf{5\text{–}10\%\text{ damaged flag leaves}}$ due to the disproportionate impact of flag leaf loss on panicle yield.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **Low / Sub-ETL ($<5\%\text{ folded leaves}$)**: Minor foliar rolling; natural parasitoids active; monitor twice weekly; no synthetic chemical required.
- **Moderate / At-ETL ($5\text{–}10\%\text{ folded leaves in vegetative / flag leaf}$)**: Threshold reached; initiate biological releases or targeted diamide application.
- **High / Severe ($>20\%\text{ folded leaves}$)**: Extensive canopy bleaching; urgent foliar spray required to prevent flag leaf destruction.

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Optimal Spacing**: Adopt wider spacing ($20\text{ cm} \times 15\text{ cm}$ or $20\text{ cm} \times 20\text{ cm}$) to avoid excessive canopy shading and dense microclimates.
- **Bund Sanitation**: Clear collateral grassy weeds (*Echinochloa colona*, *Leersia hexandra*, *Paspalum*) from field bunds and irrigation channels as they serve as alternate breeding hosts.
- **Balanced Nitrogen Management**: Avoid excess urea applications; apply nitrogen in 3 to 4 split doses synchronized with crop growth stages.
- **Rope Dragging (கயிறு இழுத்தல்)**: In early vegetative stages, drag a thorny coir rope across the crop canopy in standing water to disturb and dislodge caterpillars into the water, where they are consumed by predatory fish and aquatic insects.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Egg Parasitoids**: Release *Trichogramma chilonis* egg parasitoids @ 50,000 per hectare (or 2.5 cc/ha) starting from 30 days after transplanting (total 3 releases at weekly intervals).
- **Larval Parasitoids**: Conserve natural braconid wasps (*Apanteles* spp., *Bracon* spp.) and ichneumonid parasitoids that attack caterpillars inside the leaf folds.
- **Predator Conservation**: Protect predatory spiders (*Lycosa*, *Tetragnatha*, *Clubiona*) and predatory beetles (*Ophionea nigrofasciata*).

## 10. Chemical Management
When pest density exceeds the Economic Threshold Level (ETL), apply approved insecticides in 500 L water/ha using a fine hollow-cone nozzle:
- **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC** @ 150 ml/ha (30 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly systemic anthranilic diamide; provides 20+ days residual control; safe to natural parasitoids; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 47 days.
- **Flubendiamide 39.35 SC** @ 50 ml/ha (10 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Ryanodine receptor modulator; rapid cessation of larval feeding; PHI: 30 days.
- **Azadirachtin 1% (10,000 ppm)** @ 1000 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Botanical antifeedant for early sub-ETL suppression.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Scouting Method**: Count total leaves and number of fresh, green folded leaves containing live larvae across 20 randomly selected hills per acre.
- **Flag Leaf Inspection**: Specifically assess flag leaves separately during panicle emergence.
- **Light Trap Catches**: Install light traps to monitor adult moth flight peaks; peak moth flights precede peak larval rolling by 7 to 10 days.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agriculture Extension Officer or KVK Agronomist if:
- **Widespread Flag Leaf Blanched Patches**: Over 15% of flag leaves rolled and bleached during head emergence.
- **Post-Spray Non-Control**: Live larvae persist inside leaf folds 4 days after diamide application (indicative of poor spray penetration or water volume deficit).
- **Concurrent Blast Outbreak**: Fungal blast lesions developing on leaf folder scraped wounds.
