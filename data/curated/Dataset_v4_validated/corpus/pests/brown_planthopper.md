# IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank: Brown Planthopper (BPH) Management

## 1. Identification
The brown planthopper (*Nilaparvata lugens*, BPH) is one of the most economically damaging sap-feeding insect pests of irrigated rice across South and Southeast Asia. 

Farmers and scouts can identify BPH through its distinct morphological features:
- **Adult Planthopper**: Small, brownish insect (3.5 to 4.5 mm long) with a yellowish-brown to dark brown body and prominent white markings on the mid-dorsal thorax. It exists in two distinct wing forms (dimorphism): long-winged (*macropterous*) adults specialized for long-distance migration, and short-winged (*brachypterous*) adults adapted for high reproduction within the local crop canopy.
- **Nymphs**: Newly emerged nymphs are tiny, translucent-white with dark eyes, turning yellowish-brown with subtle white bands across the abdominal segments as they mature through five instars.
- **Egg Clusters**: Crescent-shaped white eggs laid in straight slits cut inside the leaf sheath or midrib in clusters of 4 to 12 eggs.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Brown planthopper infestation is distinguished from other paddy pests through specific micro-habitat and diagnostic traits:
- **Location on Plant**: BPH congregates almost exclusively at the base of rice tillers just 2 to 10 cm above the standing water line, whereas Green Leafhoppers (*Nephotettix virescens*) and Leaf Folders inhabit the upper leaf canopy.
- **Hopper Burn Pattern**: BPH feeding damage spreads outwards in distinct circular patches ("hopper burn") where plants turn yellow, brown, and dry up as if scorched by fire. In contrast, nutrient deficiency causes uniform field-wide discoloration rather than localized circular patches.
- **Honeydew & Sooty Mold**: Dense BPH colonies excrete large quantities of sticky honeydew, causing black sooty mold fungus to colonize the lower stems and water surface.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Direct Sap Depletion**: Thousands of nymphs and adults pierce the phloem tissue at the base of tillers and suck plant sap, depriving the rice plant of carbohydrates and water.
- **Hopper Burn (புகையான் கருகல்)**: Infested plants exhibit rapid yellowing, wilting, browning, and complete lodging (collapse) in circular patches. Unchecked infestations expand rapidly across the field within 3 to 5 days.
- **Grain Filling Impairment**: Feeding during booting and flowering leads to severe panicle choking, unfilled grains, and high grain sterility.
- **Secondary Viral Vector Role**: Transmits grassy stunt virus and ragged stunt virus in endemic regions.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Oviposition & Fecundity**: A single female lays 100 to 300 eggs inside leaf sheath tissues over her lifespan. Incubation takes 7 to 9 days.
- **Nymphal Development**: Nymphs pass through 5 instars over 12 to 15 days, actively feeding at the tiller base.
- **Adult Longevity & Dispersal**: Adults live for 10 to 20 days. High nitrogen fertilizer and continuous deep flooding trigger rapid brachypterous population explosions.
- **Resurgence Risk**: Indiscriminate foliar spraying of broad-spectrum synthetic pyrethroids (e.g. Cypermethrin, Deltamethrin) decimates natural spider and mirid bug populations, inducing severe BPH resurgence.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
BPH infests rice crops from the late vegetative stage through maturity:
- **Seedling Stage**: Early nursery infestation in waterlogged beds.
- **Active Tillering to Maximum Tillering**: Population builds up unnoticed at the base of dense tillers.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Highly vulnerable period where feeding causes extensive tiller mortality.
- **Flowering & Milking Stage**: Peak danger zone for catastrophic "hopper burn" outbreaks.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Field chemical interventions must be strictly based on pest density per hill across growth stages:
- **Seedling Stage**: $\ge 1\text{ to }2\text{ nymphs per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
- **Vegetative Stage**: $\ge 5\text{ to }10\text{ nymphs per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Predator Modifier*: The threshold increases to $10\text{–}15\text{ nymphs/hill}$ if beneficial natural predators (mirid bugs *Cyrtorhinus lividipennis* or wolf spiders *Lycosa pseudoannulata*) are present at $\ge 1\text{ per hill}$.
- **Reproductive Stage**: $\ge 10\text{ to }20\text{ nymphs per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Context Modifier*: Upper threshold ($20/\text{hill}$) applies when natural biological control is intact.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **Low / Sub-ETL ($<5\text{ nymphs/hill}$)**: Trace population; monitor weekly; avoid all broad-spectrum pyrethroid sprays.
- **Moderate / At-ETL ($5\text{–}10\text{ nymphs/hill}$)**: Drain standing water; open "alleyways" (pathways) in the field to increase airflow and sunlight penetration.
- **High / Severe ($>10\text{ nymphs/hill}$ or Initial Hopper Burn Patches)**: Critical emergency; apply targeted Insect Growth Regulators (IGR) directing spray to tiller bases.

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant & Tolerant Cultivars**: Plant resistant paddy varieties suited for South India, such as ADT 36, ASD 16, CO 42, and PY 3.
- **Formation of Alleyways (பாதை அமைத்தல்)**: Create 30 cm wide alleyways after every 8 to 10 rows of planted rice (running east-west) to allow sunlight and aeration into the lower canopy, creating microclimates unfavorable for BPH.
- **Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)**: Drain standing water from the field for 2 to 4 days during early infestation to expose nymphs to desiccation and natural predators.
- **Balanced Nitrogen Application**: Split nitrogen fertilizer into 3 to 4 balanced doses; avoid excess urea top-dressing during humid periods.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Predatory Mirid Bug**: Conserve *Cyrtorhinus lividipennis* (mirid bug), which actively preys on BPH eggs and young nymphs.
- **Spider Predators**: Conserve wolf spiders (*Lycosa pseudoannulata*), lynx spiders (*Oxyopes* spp.), and orb-weavers, each capable of consuming 10 to 20 hoppers daily.
- **Entomopathogenic Fungi**: Spray *Beauveria bassiana* or *Metarhizium anisopliae* formulations during early humid stages.

## 10. Chemical Management
When pest density crosses the Economic Threshold Level (ETL), apply specialized hopper insecticides using high-volume sprayers directed strictly at the base of plants:
- **Buprofezin 25 SC** @ 400–800 ml/ha (160–320 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly effective chitin synthesis inhibitor (IGR); suppresses nymphal molting; safe to spiders and mirid bugs; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 30 days.
- **Pymetrozine 50 WG** @ 300 g/ha (120 g/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Selective feeding blocker; immediate cessation of sap-feeding; safe to pollinators.
- **Prohibited Chemicals**: Never use synthetic pyrethroids (Cypermethrin, Lambda-cyhalothrin) as they trigger catastrophic pest resurgence.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Base-Scouting Method**: Part the rice canopy gently and inspect the base of 20 randomly selected hills along a diagonal line once every 3 days.
- **White Sheet Tap Test**: Tap the base of 5 hills over a white plastic sheet or enameled tray to count dislodged nymphs and adult hoppers.
- **Light Trap Catches**: Monitor light traps; sudden spikes in adult catches indicate mass emergence or migratory influx.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agricultural Officer or KVK Entomology Specialist if:
- **Rapidly Expanding Hopper Burn**: Circular patches expand beyond 2 meters in diameter within 48 hours despite field drainage.
- **Chemical Non-Response**: Hoppers remain active 4 days after Buprofezin or Pymetrozine application (suspected adulteration, incorrect nozzle placement, or resistance).
- **Secondary Virus Symptoms**: Appearance of stunted tillers with twisted, ragged leaves (Rice Ragged Stunt Virus).
