# ICAR Rice Production Manual: Green Leafhopper (GLH) Management

## 1. Identification
The green leafhopper (*Nephotettix virescens*, GLH) is a major phloem-feeding pest of rice across India, holding critical economic importance as the primary biological vector of the devastating Rice Tungro Virus (RTV).

Farmers and scouts can identify the insect in the field:
- **Adult Leafhopper**: Slender, wedge-shaped, bright green insect (3 to 5 mm long). The male has a distinct black patch in the middle of each forewing and black apical wing margins, whereas the female is entirely green without the central black spot.
- **Nymphs**: Soft, pale yellow to yellowish-green nymphs without wings, moving sideways in a characteristic crab-like motion when disturbed on the leaf surface.
- **Eggs**: Elongated, cylindrical, pale yellow eggs laid in diagonal rows of 10 to 30 within the leaf sheath tissues.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Green leafhopper is distinguished from other rice hoppers and sap-sucking pests by the following field cues:
- **Canopy Habitat**: GLH feeds primarily on the upper surface of leaf blades and leaf sheaths in the upper canopy, unlike Brown Planthopper (BPH) which congregates at the submerged tiller base.
- **Locomotion**: Adults and nymphs exhibit rapid sideways scurrying and extreme jumping agility when the crop canopy is brushed.
- **Vector Association with Tungro**: Feeding by viruliferous GLH causes rapid stunting, reduced tillering, and characteristic golden-yellow to orange leaf discoloration, whereas non-viruliferous feeding causes only mild apical tip yellowing.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
- **Direct Feeding Injury**: Sap extraction by large GLH populations causes leaf tip yellowing, marginal necrosis, and reduced vigor in young seedlings.
- **Rice Tungro Virus Transmission (Critical Risk)**: GLH is the exclusive insect vector transmitting Rice Tungro Bacilliform Virus (RTBV) and Rice Tungro Spherical Virus (RTSV). Inoculation occurs within minutes of feeding. Infested plants show stunted growth, delayed flowering, sterile panicles, and orange-yellow leaf discoloration starting from the tips.
- **Honeydew Excretion**: Light honeydew droplets on upper leaves, occasionally encouraging mild sooty mold.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Fecundity & Incubation**: A female lays 200 to 300 eggs inside leaf sheaths over a 2-week period. Incubation lasts 6 to 8 days.
- **Nymphal Instars**: Nymphs pass through 5 instars over 14 to 18 days, feeding actively on phloem sap.
- **Vector Dynamics**: Both nymphs and adults acquire the Tungro virus from infected plants in 5 to 30 minutes and can transmit it semi-persistently for 5 to 7 days without passing it to their progeny.
- **Climatic Preference**: Thrives in warm, humid weather ($25\text{–}30^\circ\text{C}$, $>75\%\text{ RH}$) during nursery and early tillering stages.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
The impact of GLH varies fundamentally depending on crop stage and Tungro virus prevalence:
- **Nursery / Seedling Stage**: Extreme danger zone for virus transmission; low vector densities can devastate an entire season.
- **Early Vegetative / Active Tillering**: Critical window where virus infection causes severe plant stunting and complete yield loss.
- **Booting to Heading Stage**: Direct sap-feeding threshold applies; virus transmission risk declines as plants mature.
- **Post-Heading Stage**: Direct feeding has minimal economic impact on grain yield.

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Thresholds for GLH are dual-tiered, depending strictly on whether the area is endemic to Rice Tungro Virus:
- **Seedling / Nursery Stage**: $\ge 5\text{ hoppers per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Tungro Endemic Modifier*: In Tungro endemic tracts or seasons, the threshold drops to $\mathbf{1\text{–}2\text{ hoppers per hill}}$.
- **Vegetative Stage**: $\ge 10\text{ to }15\text{ hoppers per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Tungro Vicinity Modifier*: If active Tungro virus symptoms are present in neighboring plots, the threshold drops to $\mathbf{2\text{ hoppers per hill}}$.
- **Reproductive Stage**: $\ge 20\text{ hoppers per hill}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED_WITH_CONTEXT`).
  - *Post-Heading Modifier*: Standard direct feeding threshold when virus transmission risk diminishes.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **Low / Sub-ETL ($<2\text{ hoppers/hill}$ in Tungro zone)**: Monitor nursery and main field twice weekly; maintain neem sprays.
- **Moderate / At-ETL ($5\text{ hoppers/hill}$ in normal zone or $2/\text{hill}$ in Tungro zone)**: Vector threshold breached; immediate vector suppression required to break virus transmission chain.
- **High / Severe ($>15\text{ hoppers/hill}$ or Initial Tungro Yellowing Stunting)**: Vector control emergency; rogue out virus-infected hills and apply systemic neonicotinoid.

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant Varieties**: Cultivate Tungro and GLH resistant cultivars recommended by ICAR/TNAU, such as CO 51, ADT 45, and IR 36.
- **Nursery Protection**: Cover nursery beds with fine nylon mosquito netting or spray neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) 10 days after sowing.
- **Roguing Infected Hills**: Uproot and bury Tungro-infected stunted yellow hills to eliminate the viral inoculum source.
- **Weed Host Eradication**: Remove collateral weed hosts (*Leersia hexandra*, *Echinochloa*, *Cyperus difformis*) from field borders and canals.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Predatory Spiders**: Protect wolf spiders (*Lycosa pseudoannulata*), lynx spiders (*Oxyopes javanus*), and dwarf spiders (*Atypena formosana*), which are voracious predators of GLH nymphs and adults.
- **Egg Parasitoids**: Conserve natural egg parasitoids such as *Oligosita* spp. and *Gonatocerus* spp.
- **Aquatic Predators**: Protect water bugs (*Microvelia douglasi*) and damselfly nymphs in flooded paddy.

## 10. Chemical Management
When vector densities cross the Economic Threshold Level, apply approved systemic insecticides in 500 L water/ha:
- **Imidacloprid 17.8 SL** @ 100–125 ml/ha (20–25 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly systemic neonicotinoid; provides rapid knockdown of GLH vectors; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 21 days.
- **Thiamethoxam 25 WG** @ 100 g/ha (40 g/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Translaminar and systemic action; safe to natural predators; PHI: 14 days.
- **Buprofezin 25 SC** @ 400–800 ml/ha in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). IGR effective against hopper nymphs.

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Sweep Net Sampling**: Conduct 10 sweeps with a standard insect sweep net across 5 field locations; count GLH per sweep.
- **Visual Hill Counting**: In early vegetative stages, inspect 20 hills per acre for nymphs and adults on upper leaf surfaces.
- **Yellow Sticky Traps & Light Traps**: Set up yellow sticky traps @ 5 per acre to detect incoming GLH flights from adjacent fields.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Escalate immediately to the District Agricultural Officer or KVK Virologist if:
- **Emergence of Stunted Orange-Yellow Patches**: Foliage turns orange-yellow with severe stunting in the vegetative phase (suspected Tungro epidemic).
- **High Vector Density in Nursery**: Over 5 hoppers per seedling in the nursery bed prior to transplanting.
- **Insecticide Non-Response**: Hoppers remain abundant 48 hours after foliar spray.
