# TNAU Rice Pest Management Guide: Stem Borer

## 1. Identification
The yellow stem borer (*Scirpophaga incertulas*) is one of the most destructive and widespread monophagous insect pests of paddy across Tamil Nadu and South India. 

Farmers and field scouts can identify the pest across its life stages:
- **Adult Moth**: The female moth is bright yellowish-brown to straw-colored with a conspicuous black spot in the center of each forewing and a distinct anal tuft of yellowish-buff hairs. The male moth is smaller, dusky brown, with less distinct wing spots.
- **Egg Mass**: Flat, oval egg masses (containing 50 to 80 eggs) covered with protective buff-colored silky hairs derived from the female anal tuft, laid near the tips of tender leaf blades.
- **Larva**: Fully grown caterpillar measures 20 to 25 mm in length, possessing a pale yellow or dirty white body with a dark brown head and prothoracic shield.
- **Pupa**: Pale white to dark brown, enclosed in a white silken cocoon constructed inside the stem near the base.

## 2. Distinguishing Cues
Stem borer damage can be reliably distinguished from other insect pests and fungal diseases through the following field cues:
- **Central Tiller Pull Test**: When a tiller showing "dead heart" is pulled upward, it slides out effortlessly from the stem base because the larva has severed the vascular bundles internally. The severed base typically exhibits brown rotting and larval frass. In contrast, tillers affected by drought or fungal root rots resist easy pulling.
- **Severed White Panicles**: "White earhead" caused by stem borer can be pulled out effortlessly from the boot/flag leaf sheath, showing a chewed stem base with frass, whereas disease-induced panicle blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae*) causes a dark lesion at the neck node with the panicle firmly attached to the culm.
- **Look-alike Distinction**: Whorl maggot damage affects leaf margins and produces jagged leaves, but does not cause complete drying of the central tiller or white panicles.

## 3. Symptoms and Field Signs
Stem borer infestation manifests as two distinct stage-dependent symptom complexes:
- **Vegetative Phase — Dead Heart (நடுக்குருத்து காய்வு)**: The caterpillar enters the leaf sheath and bores into the lower stem, feeding on internal tissues. The central unfurled leaf whorl turns yellow, withers, dries up, and dies.
- **Reproductive Phase — White Earhead (வெண்கதிர்)**: The caterpillar bores into the peduncle or upper culm just prior to or during panicle emergence. The flow of water and nutrients to the inflorescence is completely cut off, resulting in an erect, empty, bleached-white panicle with 100% sterile grains (chaffy).
- **Secondary Field Signs**: Small entry/exit holes on the lower culm with visible accumulation of brownish granular frass (excreta) at the leaf axils and base of tillers.

## 4. Life-Cycle and Relevant Biology
- **Oviposition**: Female moths lay 2 to 3 egg masses on upper leaf surfaces near the tips during early night hours. Incubation lasts 5 to 8 days.
- **Larval Period**: Newly hatched larvae wander on leaf blades, suspend by silken threads to disperse to adjacent tillers, bore into leaf sheaths, and enter the stem. Larval stage undergoes 5 to 6 instars over 20 to 30 days.
- **Pupation**: Occurs inside the rice stem at the water line; pupal period lasts 6 to 10 days before the adult emerges through a pre-cut exit hole.
- **Seasonality & Diapause**: Peak activity occurs during wet and humid conditions (October to January in Tamil Nadu Samba/Thaladi seasons). Overwinters as larvae in rice stubble during off-seasons.

## 5. Vulnerable Growth Stages
Stem borer attacks rice crops across all vegetative and reproductive phases:
- **Nursery / Seedling Stage**: Egg masses laid on tender leaves; early tiller entry.
- **Early Tillering to Maximum Tillering**: Prime window for "dead heart" formation and tiller mortality.
- **Panicle Initiation & Booting Stage**: Highly critical window where stem boring severs the developing inflorescence.
- **Flowering & Grain Filling Stage**: Culmination of damage appearing as conspicuous "white earheads."

## 6. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Field chemical or biological interventions should only be triggered when systematic field scouting confirms that populations have reached or exceeded the following source-quoted thresholds:
- **ETL Classification**: `NUMERIC_ETL`
- **Vegetative Stage**: $\ge 1\text{ egg mass per m}^2$ OR $\ge 10\%\text{ dead hearts}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
- **Reproductive Stage**: $\ge 1\text{ egg mass per m}^2$ OR $\ge 5\%\text{ white ears}$ (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
- *Distinction from Severity*: ETL represents the specific pest density or damage percentage at which control costs equal yield loss prevented. Severity measures the cumulative physiological crop damage across early, moderate, and severe stages.

## 7. Severity Indicators
- **A. Observable Severity Indicators**:
  - *Early Severity*: Scattered yellowing leaves with isolated dead heart shoots that pull out easily ($<5\%$ dead hearts); minimal egg mass activity.
  - *Moderate Severity*: Noticeable dead hearts across tillers ($6\%\text{–}10\%$) and initial white earhead formation; active larval boring inside stem base.
  - *Severe Severity*: Widespread tiller death and prominent erect white panicles devoid of grain ($11\%\text{–}25\%$ dead hearts or $6\%\text{–}15\%$ white ears); substantial yield reduction underway.
  - *Catastrophic*: $>25\%$ dead hearts or $>15\%$ white ears with total panicle sterility across the field.
- **B. Quantitative Severity Thresholds**:
  - *Standard Evaluation System (IRRI/ICAR SES Scale 1–9 for Stem Borer)*:
    - SES 1–3 (Early): $<5\%$ dead hearts (vegetative) or $<1\%$ white ears (reproductive) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 5 (Moderate): $6\%\text{–}10\%$ dead hearts (vegetative) or $1\%\text{–}5\%$ white ears (reproductive) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 7 (Severe): $11\%\text{–}25\%$ dead hearts (vegetative) or $6\%\text{–}15\%$ white ears (reproductive) (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
    - SES 9 (Catastrophic): $>25\%$ dead hearts or $>15\%$ white ears (`SOURCE_SUPPORTED`).
  - *Threshold Status*: `SOURCE_SUPPORTED` (IRRI SES 5th Ed. / ICAR PoP).
  - *Internal Engine Penalty Mapping*: Early = 30, Moderate = 55, Severe = 80 (`PROJECT_DERIVED_RULE` for Farm Health Score Active Problem Load sub-index).

## 8. Cultural and Mechanical Management
- **Resistant & Tolerant Cultivars**: Cultivate recommended regional rice varieties possessing moderate stem borer tolerance, such as CO 51, ADT 43, and IR 20.
- **Seedling Tip Clipping**: Clip the top 2 to 3 inches of seedling leaf tips before transplanting to eliminate hidden egg masses.
- **Stubble Management**: Conduct deep summer plowing immediately after harvest to uproot stubble, exposing diapausing larvae and pupae to sunlight and predatory birds.
- **Balanced Nutrition**: Avoid excessive or split applications of nitrogenous fertilizers, which create succulent foliage attractive to ovipositing moths.

## 9. Biological Management
- **Egg Parasitoids**: Release *Trichogramma japonicum* egg parasitoids @ 50,000 per hectare (or 2.5 cc/ha) weekly, starting from 30 days after transplanting (total 3 to 4 releases).
- **Natural Predator Conservation**: Conserve generalist predators in the paddy ecosystem, including wolf spiders (*Lycosa pseudoannulata*), long-jawed spiders (*Tetragnatha* spp.), and carabid ground beetles.
- **Pheromone Traps**: Install yellow stem borer sex pheromone traps (Scirpo-lure) @ 12 traps per hectare for population monitoring and mass trapping of male moths.

## 10. Chemical Management
When pest density exceeds the Economic Threshold Level (ETL), apply approved insecticides with adequate water volume (500 L/ha for conventional knapsack sprayers):
- **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC** @ 150 ml/ha (30 ml/acre) in 500 L water (`VERIFIED_CURRENT`). Highly effective anthranilic diamide; safe to natural predators; Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 47 days.
- **Carbofuran 3G** @ 33 kg/ha at planting or early tillering (`RESTRICTED`). *Regulatory Warning: Class Ib high-toxicity carbamate under state-level bans/restrictions across India due to severe avian and aquatic runoff hazards. Requires explicit agronomist safety warning; safer CIBRC-registered diamides preferred.*

## 11. Monitoring Guidance
- **Scouting Protocol**: Inspect 20 fixed hills per acre along a 'W' or zigzag walking pattern once every 3 to 4 days during tillering and booting.
- **Scouting Metrics**: Count total tillers and number of dead hearts per hill; inspect 1 m² foliage zones for egg masses.
- **Trap Monitoring**: Check pheromone and light traps daily; a catch of $\ge 25\text{ moths/trap/night}$ indicates peak emergence requiring field treatment within 3 to 5 days.

## 12. Escalation and Decision Cues
Farmers and extension scouts should escalate the case to the District Assistant Director of Agriculture (ADA) or KVK Agronomist under the following conditions:
- **Resurgence Post-Spray**: Dead heart counts continue to rise 5 days after chemical application (suspected insecticide resistance or poor spray coverage).
- **Ambiguous Symptoms**: Inability to pull dead hearts easily, indicating potential bacterial foot rot or root-knot nematode damage.
- **Widespread Regional Outbreak**: Over 20% white ears observed across contiguous village holdings during panicle emergence.
